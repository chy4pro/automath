TEXT FOR THE CLEAN-ROOM AGENTS:
Let p be a large prime, G = SL2(F_p), and for integers a, b ∈ [1, N] (N ≈ p^{1/20}, or any N = p^{θ} with small fixed θ > 0) let U(t) = [[1, t],[0, 1]] and W = [[0, -1],[1, 0]] (so W U(t) W^{-1} is lower unipotent). Consider the probability measure μ on G that is uniform on the N² products h_{a,b} = U(-b) · W · U(a) (counted with multiplicity), and its convolution powers μ^{(r)} = μ * ... * μ (r factors). A "central envelope" is a bound of the form μ^{(r)}(g) ≤ B · ν(g) for all g ∈ G, where ν is a probability measure that is constant on conjugacy classes (a class function), and B ≥ 1 is a constant. Goal: for as small a fixed r as you can (r = 2, 3, 4, …), prove a central envelope with B ≤ C · p^{9/20} for an absolute constant C (or any B ≤ p^{1/2 − δ}). Equivalent formulation: show that the mass μ^{(r)} puts on any single conjugacy class 𝒞 is at most B·|𝒞|/|G| times a class-independent factor, AND is spread within the class up to a factor B. Tools you may use and must prove from scratch or state precisely: counting solutions of matrix equations with entries in short intervals, character/trace considerations (the trace of h_{a,b}-products as a polynomial in a, b), Cauchy–Schwarz, elementary bounds on the number of representations of a matrix as such a product. If the goal is out of reach, prove the strongest pointwise or class-mass bound you can (state B as a function of p, N, r) and identify the exact obstruction (which g or which classes carry too much mass, and why).
