TEXT FOR THE CLEAN-ROOM AGENTS:
Let p be a large prime, G = SL2(F_p). Let μ be a symmetric probability measure on G (μ(g) = μ(g^{-1})). Write E(μ) = ||μ||_2^2 − 1/|G| = Σ_g μ(g)^2 − 1/|G| (the excess ℓ² mass) and μ*μ for convolution. Assume: (i) non-concentration: μ(gH) ≤ p^{-τ} for every coset gH of every proper subgroup H ≤ G, for a fixed τ > 0 (you may take τ = 1/24); (ii) p^{-2+2c} ≤ E(μ) ≤ p^{-h} for some fixed h > 0 (the measure is far from uniform but not too spread). Goal: prove that E(μ*μ) ≤ p^{-2c} · E(μ) — i.e. one convolution multiplies the excess ℓ² mass by p^{-2c} — with an explicit constant c > 0 as large as you can, valid for all large p and all such μ. Target values: c ≥ 1/468 is useful, c ≥ 1/120 is very valuable, c ≥ 1/40 would be spectacular. Give a complete proof of whatever you can prove (every inequality justified), state exactly where the exponent is lost, and if you cannot prove it, give the best partial statement you can prove and the obstruction. Standard facts about SL2(F_p) (subgroup structure: Borel, dihedral, exceptional; orders; representation theory) may be used and must be stated precisely when used.
