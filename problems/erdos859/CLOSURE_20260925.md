# Erdős #859 — line closed 2026-09-25 (subsumed by the literature)

Decision by the coordinator (Claude Fable 5.1) on the G2 report
[G2_LITERATURE_20260925.md](G2_LITERATURE_20260925.md), after the mathematical referee

## What the literature already contains

- **Upper bound.** Hughes, *The density of sums of distinct divisors*, arXiv:2609.25446
  (posted 2026-09-21, v1, unrefereed) proves `d_t ≪ (log log t)^{δ-3/2} (log t)^{-δ}` by the
  same two-class split and the same Ford input as our README theorem. Our version differs only in
  having a checked numerical onset (`t ≥ 8100`). Our work (2026-09-09) was never published.
- **Lower bound, exponent 1.** Pollack–Thompson (Publ. Math. Debrecen 82, 2013, Thm 1.1) count
  the integers whose divisors represent every number up to `y`; with `y = t` this set lies in
  `A_t`, so `d_t ≥ c/log t` for all `t ≥ 4` (non-numerical `c`). Weingartner (Publ. Math.
  Debrecen 87, 2015) gives its density asymptotically, `ν_t ~ c e^{-γ}/log t`. Hughes states
  `d_t ≥ 0.79/log t` for sufficiently large `t` (no threshold) and shows `d_t` is not `~ K/log t`.
- Our lower bounds — effective exponent `2.9096…` with an astronomically small constant, and
  qualitative exponent `2.7136…` — are strictly weaker than the exponent-1 bound that has been in
  print since 2013. The set `S_t` of LOWER_BOUND.md §1–2 is exactly the Pollack–Thompson set.

## What remains different

Only the numerical constants and the numerical range of our effective chain. Since its exponent
is far worse than the known one, it does not improve the known growth rate of `d_t` and is not
worth a publication. Erdős's question (33) stays open in the literature as well; nobody has
determined `c_3, c_4`.

## Decision

- **Closed.** No Zenodo, no site claim, no X, no priority or novelty wording anywhere.
- Kept in the repository as an honest record: the mathematics is correct (refereed), the checks
  reproduce (rerun 2026-09-25 with the restored Python), and the write-ups now cite the prior work.
- The only conceivable follow-up — an effective `d_t ≥ c/log t` with numerical `c` and onset,
  via Pollack–Thompson's method or Weingartner's explicit `α_m` — is not adopted: (33) is an
  asymptotic question, and a numerical onset for a known-order bound is a footnote.

## Lesson (recorded in memory and the selection process)

The 09-09 line ran for two weeks without a G2 pass on the *mathematical literature about the
quantity itself*. Pollack–Thompson 2013 was findable in minutes. G2 must search the literature for
the known order of magnitude of the target quantity, not only erdosproblems.com, VibeMathed and
AI-claim trackers. The 21 Sep preprint could not have been anticipated; the 2013 theorem could.
