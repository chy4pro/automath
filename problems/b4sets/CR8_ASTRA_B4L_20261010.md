## 1. Exact partial theorem

Write q = u − 1 and use binomial coefficients C(m,j) = 0 when j > m. Set

- K4 = 3 C(m,4);
- K2 = 3 C(m,3);
- K1 = C(m,2);
- K = K4 + K2 + K1 = m(m−1)(m²−m+2)/8.

For an integer k ≥ 0 define t = min(k,u−1) and

H_u(k) = tu − t(t+1)/2.

**Theorem 1.** For every N ≥ 256 and every B4 set A in {0,...,N−1}, the following three lower bounds hold:

(1) D(A,N) ≥ 4u(u−1) − 2[2H_u(K4) + H_u(K4+K2) + H_u(K)].

(2) D(A,N) ≥ 2h4(h4+1), where h4 = max(0,u−1−K4).

(3) D(A,N) ≥ 4h(h+1), where h = max(0,u−1−K).

Consequently the target 100D(A,N) ≥ u(u−1) holds if either of these conditions holds:

(4) 14K4 ≤ 13(u−1);

(5) 20K ≤ 19(u−1).

In particular it holds whenever |A| ≤ 6. Another, simpler sufficient condition is

(6) 5m⁴ ≤ 38(u−1).

These conditions are sufficient, not necessary. Formula (1) sometimes certifies more cases than (4) or (5). The theorem is not claimed outside these conditions unless formula (1) itself reaches the target.

For context, the exact cardinality packing inequality

(7) m(m−1)(m²−m+6) ≤ 16(N−1)

also holds. It does not close the gap between the sufficient conditions above and all possible B4 sets.

## 2. Correction of a supplied fact

The brief's assertion that S = A+A is Sidon is false for every A with at least two elements.

For distinct a,b in A,

(2a)+(2b) = (a+b)+(a+b),

and {2a,2b} and {a+b,a+b} are distinct multisets of elements of S. All three indicated elements of S are distinct.

The set A = {0,1} is already a B4 example: a four-term sum is determined by the number of 1s in it. Its sumset is {0,1,2}, with 0+2 = 1+1.

An additional original check used A = {0,1,5}: all C(6,4) = 15 nondecreasing four-term representations have different sums. Its complete nonzero T histogram is

x:    −10 −9 −8 −6 −3 −2   2  3  6  8  9 10
T(x):   1  2  1  2  2  1   1  2  2  1  2  1.

Thus the proposed Sidon reduction cannot be used as stated. This correction does not by itself refute B4-L.

## 3. Representation uniqueness and the exact histogram counts

If A is nonempty, its B4 property implies its Bh property for h = 1,2,3: pad an h-term equality on both sides with 4−h copies of any fixed element of A, apply B4, and cancel the padding. The empty set causes no difficulty: D = 4u(u−1).

Represent a multiset on A by its vector of multiplicities. Suppose two disjoint unordered pair representations give the same nonzero difference:

P−Q = R−S numerically,

where P,Q,R,S are multisets of size two, and the supports of P,Q are disjoint, as are those of R,S. The numerical equality P+S = R+Q is an equality of four-term sums. B4 implies equality of the multiplicity vectors:

P−Q = R−S as vectors.

The positive and negative parts of a signed vector are unique. Disjointness therefore implies P = R and Q = S. A disjoint pair-pair difference cannot be zero, since B2 would then give P = Q.

For one disjoint unordered representation, its number of ordered representations is the product of the numbers of orderings of its two pairs. Each factor is either one or two. Therefore T(x) belongs to {0,1,2,4}, and the values 1,2,4 have the following exact counts on positive x.

- T(x)=4: choose four distinct elements, then an unordered bipartition into two pairs. There are three bipartitions. The pair sums are unequal by B2, so exactly one orientation gives positive x. This gives K4 = 3C(m,4).
- T(x)=2: choose three distinct elements, then choose which element is doubled. The other two form the distinct pair. Again exactly one orientation is positive. This gives K2 = 3C(m,3).
- T(x)=1: choose two distinct elements and double both. This gives K1 = C(m,2).

The uniqueness proved above shows that none of these choices collide, including choices in different categories. Swapping the two pairs gives T(−x)=T(x).

The formula for K follows by expansion:

3C(m,4)+3C(m,3)+C(m,2)
= m(m−1)[(m−2)(m−3)+4(m−2)+4]/8
= m(m−1)(m²−m+2)/8.

In particular, T≤4 is valid. Merely knowing that individual pair sums have ordered multiplicity at most two would not, by itself, prove this bound; the B4 uniqueness argument is essential.

### Ordinary differences and packing

Let P = (A−A)\{0}. By B2, every x in P has a unique ordered representation x=a−b. In particular, there are C(m,2) positive elements of P.

The sets P and {x:T(x)>0} are disjoint. Otherwise a disjoint pair-pair difference would equal a−b. This is a three-term equality; B3 would identify the reduced signed multiplicity vectors, whose positive parts have sizes two and one, respectively. That is impossible.

All positive elements of P are at most N−1. Every positive x with T(x)>0 is at most 2N−2. Their disjoint union therefore has at most 2N−2 elements:

K + C(m,2) ≤ 2N−2.

Substituting the formula for K and multiplying by eight proves (7). Also, independently, B4 gives C(m+3,4) ≤ 4N−3 by placing the distinct four-multiset sums in {0,...,4N−4}.

## 4. Proof of Theorem 1

Symmetry and the finite arithmetic progression sum give the exact identity

D = 2 Σ_{x=1}^{u−1} (4−T(x))(u−x)
  = 4u(u−1) − 2 Σ_{x=1}^{u−1} T(x)(u−x).

For a subset E of the positive integers with at most k elements, the sum of the weights (u−x) over E∩{1,...,u−1} is at most H_u(k). To see this, put its r elements in increasing order e_1<...<e_r. Then e_i≥i, so

Σ_{i=1}^r (u−e_i) ≤ Σ_{i=1}^r(u−i) ≤ H_u(k).

The second inequality holds because r≤min(k,u−1) and every extra summand u−i up to that limit is positive.

For every x, the identity

T(x) = 2·1_{T(x)=4} + 1_{T(x)≥2} + 1_{T(x)≥1}

holds by checking its four possible values. On the positive integers these three sets have cardinalities K4, K4+K2, and K. Apply the preceding weight inequality to each of them, multiply by their coefficients, and substitute in the exact identity for D. This proves (1).

At least h4 of the u−1 positive integers below u do not have T=4. Each has deficit 4−T≥2. Among h4 distinct positions the smallest possible sum of positive weights is 1+...+h4 = h4(h4+1)/2. Accounting for negative x as well gives

D ≥ 2 · 2 · h4(h4+1)/2 = 2h4(h4+1),

which proves (2). Similarly, at least h positions have T=0, hence deficit four. The same argument gives

D ≥ 2 · 4 · h(h+1)/2 = 4h(h+1),

proving (3).

If (4) holds, then h4≥q/14. The function z(z+1) is increasing on z≥0, because its difference between z'≥z and z is (z'−z)(z'+z+1)≥0. Thus

D ≥ 2(q/14)(q/14+1)
  = q(q+14)/98
  = (u−1)(u+13)/98
  ≥ u(u−1)/100.

The last inequality follows from 100(u+13)−98u = 2u+130 > 0.

If (5) holds, then h≥q/20, giving

D ≥ 4(q/20)(q/20+1)
  = q(q+20)/100
  = (u−1)(u+19)/100
  ≥ u(u−1)/100.

For m≥1,

m⁴−m(m−1)(m²−m+2)
= m(2m²−3m+2)
= m[(m−1)(2m−1)+1] > 0.

Consequently K≤m⁴/8; the same inequality is immediate for m=0. Condition (6) therefore implies (5), since

20K ≤ (5/2)m⁴ ≤ 19q.

Finally u≥70 for every N≥256. This follows from monotonicity and the exact calculation

69^17 = 18215225100020464195180876374789
< 256^13 = 20282409603651670423947251286016
≤ 70^17 = 23263051398720700000000000000000.

If m≤6, then K4≤3C(6,4)=45, and

14K4 ≤ 630 ≤ 897 = 13·69 ≤ 13(u−1).

Thus (4) applies. This finishes the proof of every asserted part of Theorem 1.

## 5. Exact energy identities and the obstruction to using them alone

Let r(s) be the ordered pair-sum count, and let

E(x) = Σ_s r(s)r(s−x).

Then

E(0)=2m²−m,

because the m doubled sums have r=1 and the C(m,2) other sums have r=2. For nonzero x the exact formula is

E(x) = T(x) + (4m−4)1_P(x).                         (8)

Indeed, a representation with a shared element cancels to an ordinary difference x=a−b. For its unique such ordered pair a,b, a common element c different from a,b gives four ordered pair-pair representations. The choices c=a and c=b give two each. The total is 4(m−2)+2+2 = 4m−4. Every representation not counted this way is disjoint and belongs to T. When P is empty the correction is absent.

The exact ordered eight-variable equality count is

Σ_x E(x)² = 24m⁴−72m³+82m²−33m.                    (9)

Here is a direct verification, rather than an appeal to an external energy formula. For a common four-multiset, square its number of orderings. Its multiplicity patterns give, respectively,

576 C(m,4)                           for 1+1+1+1,
144 m C(m−1,2)                      for 2+1+1,
36 C(m,2)                           for 2+2,
16 m(m−1)                           for 3+1,
m                                   for 4.

Their sum expands to (9). B4 ensures that there are no contributions from two distinct four-multisets. The same sum counts ΣE² by rearranging the eight variables.

In terms of T itself,

Σ_x T(x)   = m⁴−4m³+6m²−3m,
Σ_x T(x)² = 4m⁴−20m³+33m²−17m.

They also follow directly from the three histogram counts:

ΣT = 2(4K4+2K2+K1),
ΣT² = 2(16K4+4K2+K1).

These identities say nothing about the positions of the positive differences once their categories are fixed.

For a concrete relaxation showing the missing information, take N=256, m=7, u=70. Assign a symmetric artificial histogram with

- T=4 at positive positions 1,...,105;
- T=2 at 106,...,210;
- T=1 at 211,...,231;
- an artificial ordinary-difference set P_+={232,...,252};
- T=0 elsewhere.

All counts K4=105, K2=105, K1=21 and |P_+|=21 are correct. All P positions are ≤255 and all T positions are ≤510. It meets the support packing inequality, has T≤4, has the exact global T moments above, and, with (8) defining E, has the exact value (9). Yet its weighted deficit at u=70 is zero.

This is NOT a B4 set, NOT a realizability claim, and NOT a counterexample to the task. It proves only that these histogram, support, and unweighted moment constraints alone do not imply the requested bound. Additional structure of actual locations a+b−c−d is required.

### A further explicit moment bound

For completeness, a weighted Cauchy–Schwarz route gives a valid additional inequality. Put

F(θ)=Σ_{a∈A} exp(2πiaθ),
J_u(θ)=|Σ_{j=0}^{u−1} exp(2πijθ)|².

All integrals here are over [0,1]. Finite Fourier expansion and the identity that the integral of exp(2πikθ) is zero for nonzero integer k give

∫|F|⁴J_u = Σ_x E(x)(u−|x|)_+,

∫|F|⁸ = 24m⁴−72m³+82m²−33m,

∫J_u² = u²+2Σ_{j=1}^{u−1}j² = (2u³+u)/3.

The last finite sum follows, for example, by summing
(j+1)³−j³=3j²+3j+1 together with the arithmetic progression formula.

Cauchy–Schwarz, (8), and the nonnegativity of the P correction imply, for m≥1,

D ≥ 4u(u−1)+(2m²−m)u
    − sqrt[(24m⁴−72m³+82m²−33m)(2u³+u)/3].         (10)

Every term is explicit. This bound is negative in, for example, the unsettled case N=256,m=7, so it supplies no positive improvement there. It was not used to claim a larger proved range.

Another attempted positivity argument gives

Σ_x T(x) exp(2πixθ)
= |F(θ)|⁴−(4m−4)|F(θ)|²+2m²−3m
= (|F(θ)|²−2m+2)²−2m²+5m−4
≥ −2m²+5m−4.

Integrating against J_u yields a lower bound on the realised weighted sum of T, whereas B4-L needs an upper bound. That is the precise direction obstruction for this elementary square-completion route.

## 6. Window counting: correct identity and current limit

The unweighted set S=A+A has size m(m+1)/2, by B2. For x≠0 let

R_S(x)=#{(s,s')∈S²:s−s'=x}.

The correct replacement for the false Sidon assertion is

R_S(x)=m·1_P(x)+1_{T(x)>0}.                        (11)

For x=a−b in P, the m representations are (a+c,b+c), c∈A. A disjoint representation cannot coexist with them, by Section 3. For x outside P, uniqueness of the unordered disjoint pairs gives either one representation or none. Notice that doubled pair sums do not change this unweighted count.

If I is any interval of L consecutive integers and n_I=|S∩I|, each positive difference of S∩I is between 1 and L−1. Summing its number of representations and using (11) gives

C(n_I,2) ≤ (L−1)+(m−1)|P∩{1,...,L−1}|.             (12)

The first term accounts for one possible representation at each position; the second allows the extra m−1 representations at ordinary differences. This is a proved upper bound, but it is not the Sidon interval bound.

For windows I_t={t,...,t+u−1}, summing over all integer t gives the exact identity

Σ_t C(|S∩I_t|,2)
= m Σ_{x∈P, 0<x<u}(u−x)
  + Σ_{0<x<u, T(x)>0}(u−x).                        (13)

A pair of points at distance x<u lies in exactly u−x such windows, which proves (13) by double counting. Only finitely many windows contribute.

This route did not produce an upper bound with a fixed positive saving for the second term in (13), let alone for its T-weighted version. Treating S∩I as Sidon drops the first correction in (12) and is invalid. The additional graph structure of S as a sumset was considered, but no inequality sufficient to replace this missing saving was established.

## 7. A genuine B4 family with complete small differences at a free window

This construction is included to distinguish a real local obstruction from the artificial histogram above. It is NOT a counterexample at the window prescribed in the task.

For every integer K≥1, set B=16K+1. For i=1,...,K define

c_i=B^(3i−2),   a_i=B^(3i−1),   b_i=B^(3i),
d_i=a_i+b_i−c_i−i,

and let A_K be the set of these 4K integers. They are distinct: within a block,

c_i<a_i<b_i<d_i<2B^(3i),

and the first point c_(i+1)=B^(3i+1) of the next block is larger than that last bound since B>2. Positivity and the strict inequality b_i<d_i follow from
a_i−c_i−i=B^(3i−2)(B−1)−i>0:
B^(3i−2)(B−1)≥B(B−1)>K≥i.

**Proposition 2.** A_K is B4, and T_{A_K}(x)=4 for every 1≤|x|≤K.

Proof of B4: Suppose two four-multisets on A_K have equal sums. Cancel their common occurrences and let n_{a_i},n_{b_i},n_{c_i},n_{d_i} be the remaining signed multiplicities. The sum of their positive coefficients is at most four; the sum of their negative coefficients in absolute value is also at most four.

Substitution gives an integer polynomial in B with constant coefficient

−Σ_i i n_{d_i},

whose absolute value is at most 8K<B. Every other coefficient has absolute value at most eight, also less than B. Its coefficients at the three powers belonging to block i are

n_{c_i}−n_{d_i},
n_{a_i}+n_{d_i},
n_{b_i}+n_{d_i}.

Since the polynomial evaluates to zero, reduce modulo B. The constant coefficient, an integer of absolute value less than B divisible by B, must be zero. Divide by B and repeat. Every coefficient must be zero.

Thus, writing t_i=n_{d_i}, we have

n_{a_i}=n_{b_i}=−t_i,   n_{c_i}=n_{d_i}=t_i,
Σ_i i t_i=0.

For every block, its positive coefficients sum to 2|t_i|. Therefore Σ_i|t_i|≤2. A nonzero integer vector with this property has either one nonzero coordinate, of size one or two, or two distinct coordinates of size one. The weighted sum Σi t_i cannot vanish in the first case. In the second, equal signs give a nonzero sum, and opposite signs would require the two distinct indices to be equal. This is impossible. Hence all t_i, and all original signed multiplicities, vanish. B4 follows.

Finally,

a_i+b_i−c_i−d_i=i,

and the four elements are distinct. Thus T(i)≥4; Section 3 gives T(i)≤4. Symmetry gives the negative values as well. This proves the proposition.

Define, solely in this paragraph, the deficit at a freely chosen window v by

D_v(A)=Σ_{1≤|x|<v}(4−T_A(x))(v−|x|).

Then D_(K+1)(A_K)=0 exactly. Consequently there is no positive universal bound of this form for an arbitrary window independent of the diameter and cardinality.

However A_K is contained in {0,...,N_K−1} with

N_K=2B^(3K).

This does not satisfy the required relation v=u(N_K): indeed

N_K^13 ≥ B^(39K) > (K+1)^17,

because B>K+1 and 39K>17. Therefore u(N_K)>K+1. The verified complete interval is too short. This is the exact obstruction to using this construction as a disproof of B4-L.

A simpler binary-digit block attempt failed earlier: if A contains distinct points x,x+d,y,y+2d, then

2(x+d)+y = 2x+(y+2d)

is a nontrivial equality of three-term sums, contradicting B3. Thus choosing block gaps 1,2,4,8 to tile a small interval is incompatible with B4, irrespective of how far apart the blocks are. No successful polynomial-diameter replacement for the large-base construction was found.

}
function windowU(N) {
