### (c) **My own coverage checker committed the defect it was built to catch — RULING BL, and this one was silent-and-FAVOURABLE**

Four defects in the guards, all caught on the first run **because every guard prints its
population before it grades** (RULING AS). Three were noisy-and-safe. **The fourth was
not**, and it is the one worth the section:

〔**The checker accepted a CROSS-REFERENCE as a STATEMENT.** GUARD B passed *Lemma TAIL*
on the line
> *"**Lemma TAIL** is stated in Appendix C, where it is used."*

**That is a pointer, not a statement — it is the exact species GUARD B exists to detect**,
and the guard scored it OK. Had the population not been printed, the guard would have
certified coverage it had not checked, on a check written *specifically* because A.1 had
certified coverage it did not have. **Fixed at the root**: pointer shapes (*"is stated
in"*, *"is printed in"*, *"see Appendix"*) are now rejected outright, and TAIL's **real**
statement block in Appendix C is what the guard has to find — which it does. **A checker
that can be satisfied by a cross-reference is the silent-and-favourable failure mode, and
it took one round to reproduce it inside the very tool built against it.**〕

The other three, recorded rather than tidied away: `\b` after `+`, `*` or `'` **never
matches** (both sides non-word), so `Lemma Z+`, `Lemma F3'` and `Lemma C*` read as MISSING
even when stated; and a `[^*]+?` lead-in **cannot cross the escaped `\*`** in
*"**Lemma C\*, (F-b).**"*, so the second name of a joint lead-in was invisible. **Both
erred against ourselves** — they would have blocked the write, not waved it through.
**Direction of error is the thing to record**, exactly as §7.40's comma-stripping defect.
