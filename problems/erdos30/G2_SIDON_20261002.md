# Sidon second-term prior-art check — 2026-10-02

## Scope and decision

The claim supplied for comparison is: every strong Sidon set \(A\subseteq[N]=\{1,\ldots,N\}\), with all unordered two-term sums **including doubles** distinct, satisfies

\[
|A|\leq\sqrt N+\frac{2\sqrt2}{3}N^{1/4}+1
\qquad(N\geq207360000).
\]

Here \(2\sqrt2/3=0.942809041582063\ldots\). This audit found no earlier statement of this bound, no strong-Sidon second-term coefficient at most \(0.9429\), and no assertion that this number is a barrier for the relevant methods. The smallest same-scope public claim located has coefficient \(0.943006169985179\ldots\) and an \(O(1)\) remainder, in the wustep repository. Its coefficient exceeds the target by approximately \(0.000197128403116\); these decimal comparisons were evaluated locally with JavaScript, not taken as proof evidence.

**The exact number does occur in a different 2026 theorem:** Gupta–O'Bryant prove an LM-ruler diameter bound with coefficient \(\sqrt{8/9}=2\sqrt2/3\). Its object, main scale and conclusion differ; see below. This prevents saying that the number itself was absent from prior literature.

CLEAR means only that the searched statements do not subsume the supplied claim. It is not an exhaustive priority guarantee, a referee report, or a comparison of the proposed proof with earlier mechanisms. The researcher did not read the local proof, selection-probe report or script, or proof-writer messages. The claim would remain a second-order upper-bound improvement, not a solution of Erdős #30.

Translation identifies Sidon subsets of \([N]\) with those of \(\{0,\ldots,N-1\}\) exactly. Distinct nonzero ordered differences are equivalent to the strong sum convention over the integers. Diameter-based papers use diameter at most \(N-1\); this shift does not change the asymptotic coefficient or an unspecified \(O(1)\). It does matter when comparing a fully explicit additive constant.

## Exact-number and method-limit checks

The literal exact-number hit is [Gupta–O'Bryant, arXiv:2605.14229v1, 2026-05-14](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.14229v1), **Definition 2 and Theorem 3**. An LM ruler permits at most \(d-1\) representations of every positive distance \(d\). For an \(n\)-element LM ruler \(\{0=\ell_1<\cdots<\ell_n\}\), they prove

\[
\ell_n\geq\sqrt{8/9}\,(n-1)^{3/2}.
\]

That is neither a Sidon second-order cardinality bound nor a barrier claim. Merely setting the paper's multiplicity parameter to 1 does not turn this LM theorem into the supplied inequality. No such transfer is asserted in the inspected statements. Its existence is numerical overlap only; mechanism comparison remains outside this isolated audit.

Targeted full-text searches covered BFR v2, O'Bryant v1/v2, CHO v1, Hou–Zhao v1/v2/v3, and wustep's problem/research/walkthrough/attack/README material. Searches included decimal fragments 0.942…, 1.885…, radical variants, “limit”, “cannot”, “barrier”, and “explicit”. General web queries also tested 0.9429, 0.942809, 207360000 and radical spellings. Relevant statements actually found:

- **O'Bryant v2, after Theorem 4:** the formerly alleged method limit is the **additive \(+1/2\)** in \(\sqrt N+N^{1/4}+1/2\); the author says it can itself be improved by an explicit \(O(1)\). It is not a limit on the \(N^{1/4}\) coefficient. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.07800v2)
- **CHO:** the inability statement in the final discussion concerns the exponent of \(g\) in a \(g\)-thin improvement \(\varepsilon_g=\Omega(g^{-2})\). It does not state a 0.942809 barrier for strong Sidon sets. [Section 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.20032v1)
- **Hou–Zhao v3:** Proposition 2.2 says averaging individually feasible scalar systems cannot outperform the best constituent; the concluding section leaves further numerical improvements possible. Neither states the target number as a floor. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01169v3)
- **wustep:** reports saturation of particular searches and says it did not reach the 0.942 level. These are numerical observations, not a universal lower bound on the method. [Research log](https://github.com/wustep/maths/blob/da2440b68979b78d201182118d30ca418e3c2001/problems/sidon-second-term/RESEARCH.md)
