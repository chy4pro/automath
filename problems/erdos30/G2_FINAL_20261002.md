## 2. Gupta–O'Bryant: the same constant in a different theorem

The official [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14229), [versioned HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.14229v1), and [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.14229v1) were read at **15:05:42–15:12:29 UTC**. Aditya Gupta and Kevin O'Bryant's *Optimal Diameters of High Multiplicity g-Golomb Rulers* currently has only **v1**, submitted **2026-05-14 00:47:08 UTC**. This is the submission timestamp; the first public announcement time was not established.

An LM ruler is an integer set \(\mathcal L\) satisfying
\[
r_{\mathcal L-\mathcal L}(d):=\#\{(x,y)\in\mathcal L^2:y-x=d\}\le d-1
\quad(d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}).
\]
Its minimum possible diameter at size \(n\) is denoted \(L(n)\). Theorem 13, section 5.3, PDF pages 11–12, states verbatim:

> For any LM ruler \(\mathcal A\) of size \(n\ge2\), we have

\[
\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal A)\ge\frac{2\sqrt2}{3}(n-1)^{3/2}.
\]

The proof orders consecutive gaps; their small-gap multiplicities imply that the \(i\)-th gap exceeds \(\sqrt{2i}\), whose integral supplies the constant. No capacity intercept or universal \(ab\ge8/9\) statement was found among the numbered results or proofs.

Here is the independent comparison, keeping the quantifiers and exponents explicit. Write \(\gamma=2\sqrt2/3\), \(k=|A|\), and \(D=\max A-\min A\).

1. Positive differences in a Sidon set have multiplicity at most one. A Sidon set with no distance 1 is therefore LM. Every Sidon set gives an LM ruler \(2A\). However, \(\{0,1\}\) is Sidon and not LM; \(\{0,3,6\}\) is LM and not Sidon, since \(0+6=3+3\). Thus the classes are related but not identical.
2. Applying Theorem 13 to \(2A\) yields
   \[
   k\le1+\left(\frac{2(N-1)}\gamma\right)^{2/3}.
   \]
   A Sidon set has at most one pair at distance 1. Delete one endpoint if needed. Applying the theorem to the remaining set, for \(k\ge3\), improves this to
   \[
   k\le2+\left(\frac{N-1}\gamma\right)^{2/3}.
   \]
   Both bounds have order \(N^{2/3}\); neither yields R1. Even \(D\ge\binom k2\), from elementary difference counting, eventually gives a stronger diameter lower bound than this LM specialization.
3. The coefficient conversion relevant to R1 is
   \[
   D\ge k^2-c k^{3/2}-O(k)
   \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
   k\le\sqrt N+\tfrac c2N^{1/4}+O(1).
   \]
   Substituting \(k=\sqrt N+uN^{1/4}+O(1)\) gives the coefficient \(2u-c\) of \(N^{3/4}\). A diameter *correction* \(c=\gamma\) would give \(\gamma/2\), whereas R1 corresponds to \(c=2\gamma=4\sqrt2/3\). The LM theorem instead has \(\gamma k^{3/2}\) as its leading diameter term, with no leading \(k^2\) and no negative correction. The conversion cannot be applied to it that way.
4. The paper's Theorem 4 applies when \(g\ge L(b-1)+1\) and then gives \(G(g,g+b)=g+2b-2\). At \(g=1\), its hypothesis excludes growing \(b\); Lemma 6 gives only \(D\ge2k-3\). Nor are the holes of every optimal Golomb ruler LM: \(A=\{0,1,4,6\}\) has all six distinct positive differences, but its holes \(\{2,3,5\}\) have a forbidden distance 1.
5. Conversely, R1 would imply the stronger diameter estimate \(D\ge k^2-2\gamma k^{3/2}-O(k)\) on the **Sidon subclass**, hence eventually the LM inequality there. It does not apply to all LM rulers. The paper also constructs LM rulers of diameter order \(n^{3/2}\), excluding an indiscriminate transfer of a Sidon-sized bound to the full LM class.

Consequently this source does **not** anticipate R1 or R2 in the statements checked, and the direct specializations/inversion above do not derive R1 from it. This is a comparison of available theorem consequences, not a claim of formal logical independence or an exclusion of every imaginable additional reduction.
