### Root route g: cyclic embedding and endpoint interaction (INFORMED, OPEN)

Σ_z C_z = kT,

### E.2. An exact finite rank-prefix inequality

Let
\[
 1\le a_1<\cdots<a_k\le N,\qquad 1\le\ell<k,
 \qquad M=\ell k-\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}2.
\]
There are exactly \(M\) pairs \(i<j\) with \(j-i\le\ell\). Define
\[
 E_\ell=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}(\ell-j+1)
                   (a_j+N-a_{k-j+1}),
\]
\[
 R_\ell=\#\{(i,j):1\le i<j\le k,\ j-i\le\ell,\
                                    a_j-a_i>M\},
\]
and
\[
 C_\ell^*
 =\sum_{\substack{1\le i<j\le k\\j-i\le\ell}}
                   (a_j-a_i-M)_+
     +\binom{R_\ell}{2}.
\]
Every strong Sidon set satisfies
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{M(M+1)}2+E_\ell+C_\ell^*
       \le\frac{N\ell(\ell+1)}2.
 }\tag{E1}
\]

**Proof.** Summation separately over the orders \(r=1,\ldots,\ell\) telescopes:
\[
 \sum_{r=1}^{\ell}\sum_{i=1}^{k-r}(a_{i+r}-a_i)
 =\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}(\ell-j+1)(a_{k-j+1}-a_j)
 =\frac{N\ell(\ell+1)}2-E_\ell. \tag{E2}
\]
The \(M\) differences on the left are distinct positive integers. Suppose \(R\) of them exceed \(M\). The other \(M-R\) terms have sum at least
\((M-R)(M-R+1)/2\). The \(R\) long terms have sum
\(RM+\sum(d-M)_+\). Therefore their total sum is at least
\[
 \frac{M(M+1)}2+\sum(d-M)_++\frac{R(R-1)}2.
\]
This proves (E1). All pairs counted here have distinct indices; the strong, diagonal-inclusive sum convention is what guarantees uniqueness of these positive differences. There is no extra factor two. \(\square\)

The binomial correction is retained rather than silently discarded. It may matter for a configuration with many long differences.

### E.3. Uniform expansion and a concrete conditional improvement

Fix any real \(s>0\). Let \(x=N^{1/4}\), assume \(k\ge\sqrt N\), and write
\[
 k=x^2+c_Nx,\qquad \ell=\lfloor sx\rfloor=sx+\delta,
 \quad -1<\delta\le0.
\]
The established Sidon bound implies \(0\le c_N\le2\) for all sufficiently large \(N\), uniformly over \(A\). Also \(1\le\ell<k\) once \(N\) is sufficiently large in terms of \(s\).

For clarity, the complete expansion of \(M\) is
\[
 M=sx^3+
 \left(sc_N+\delta-\frac{s^2}{2}\right)x^2+
 \left(\delta c_N-s\delta-\frac s2\right)x
 -\frac{\delta(\delta+1)}2.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \frac{N\ell(\ell+1)}2-\frac{M(M+1)}2
 =s^2\left(\frac s2+\frac1{2s}-c_N\right)x^5
   +O_s(x^4). \tag{E3}
\]
The terms involving \(\delta x^5\) cancel. The error is uniform over all sets under discussion.

One explicit error bound, valid for \(x\ge1\), \(0\le c_N\le2\), and \(-1\le\delta\le0\), is
\[
 |\text{error in (E3)}|\le20(s+1)^4x^4.

To check this, write \(M=sx^3+Ax^2+Bx+C\). With \(d=s+1\ge1\), one has
\[
 |A|\le2d^2,\qquad |B|\le2d,\qquad |C|\le1.
\]
After the two leading terms of \(M^2\) are removed, its absolute remainder is at most
\(\bigl((2d^2+2d+1)^2+2s(2d+1)\bigr)x^4\le31d^4x^4\).
The contribution from \(M\), after division by two, is at most \(3d^4x^4\); the remaining term from \(N\ell(\ell+1)/2\) is at most \(d^4x^4/2\). The stated constant 20 is therefore sufficient.

Combining (E1) and (E3) gives the explicit uniform inequality
\[

       +\frac{20(s+1)^4}{s^2N^{1/4}}. \tag{E4}
\]

### E.4. A genuinely shared subinterval difference budget

There is also a finite inequality that couples subinterval counts without optimizing separate scalar inequalities.

Let \(I_1,\ldots,I_J\) be pairwise disjoint integer intervals, each containing \(H\ge1\) consecutive integers. Put
\[
 m_j=|A\cap I_j|,\qquad K=\sum_{j=1}^Jm_j.
\]
For every integer \(T\ge1\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{j=1}^Jm_j^2
 \le(H+T-1)\left(1+\frac{K-1}{T}\right).
 }\tag{E6}
\]

**Proof.** Translate each row \(A\cap I_j\) into \(\{0,\ldots,H-1\}\), and let \(v_j(t)\) count the translated intervals of \(T\) consecutive integers covering \(t\). Then
\[
 \sum_t v_j(t)=m_jT,\qquad
 \sum_t v_j(t)^2
 =\sum_{a,b\in A\cap I_j}(T-|a-b|)_+.
\]
Its support has at most \(H+T-1\) positions. Hence Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 T^2\sum_jm_j^2\le(H+T-1)\sum_{j,t}v_j(t)^2.
\]
The diagonal contribution is \(KT\). Because the original intervals are disjoint, each within-row ordered pair is counted once, and all nonzero differences across all rows remain distinct. Nonnegativity allows the single common upper bound
\[
 \sum_{j,t}v_j(t)^2
 \le KT+\sum_{d\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}}(T-|d|)_+
 =KT+T^2-T.
\]
Combining and dividing by \(T^2\) proves (E6). If \(K=0\), the upper energy budget is \(T(T-1)\ge0\), so empty rows and empty configurations cause no exception. \(\square\)

Applying this formula to overlapping subintervals without controlling pair-coverage multiplicities would be invalid. No improvement from a hierarchy of (E6) is claimed here.

### E.5. A fully realizable obstruction to interval-cardinality envelopes alone

The following result includes every subinterval, not just finitely many prescribed scales.

**Proposition.** For every integer \(N\ge256\), there exists an integer point set
\(B_N\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}\) such that
\[
 |B_N|=\left\lfloor\sqrt N+\gamma N^{1/4}\right\rfloor, \tag{E7}
\]
while every integer subinterval \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}\) containing \(H\ge1\) consecutive integers satisfies
\[
 |B_N\cap I|\le\sqrt H+\gamma H^{1/4}+1. \tag{E8}
\]
The constructed \(B_N\) is not Sidon.

Thus all these local upper bounds, all additivity and overlap consistency conditions for interval counts, and the fact that the counts come from distinct integer points do not imply a second-order coefficient smaller than \(\gamma\).

**Construction.** Write
\[
 q=\frac{3\gamma}{4}=\frac1{\sqrt2},\qquad
 w=N^{5/8},\qquad Q=qN^{1/4},
\]
\[
 \rho=N^{-1/2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}N^{-3/4}>0,
 \qquad B(t)=\sqrt t+\gamma t^{1/4}\quad(t\ge0).
\]
Define a positive measure on \([0,N]\) by
\[
 d\mu_N(t)=
 \left(\rho+\frac Qw
       \mathbf1_{[0,w]\cup[N-w,N]}(t)\right)dt. \tag{E9}
\]
The two end bands are disjoint because \(w\le N/8\) when \(N\ge256\). Their added masses are \(Q\) each. The total mass is exactly
\[
 \rho N+2Q=\sqrt N+\gamma N^{1/4}=B(N). \tag{E10}
\]

We first prove, for every real subinterval \(J\subseteq[0,N]\) of length \(h\), that
\[
 \mu_N(J)\le B(h). \tag{E11}
\]
The case \(h=0\) is immediate.

If \(0<h\le N/4\), the interval intersects at most one end band. Its background mass is at most
\[
 \rho h\le h/\sqrt N\le\sqrt h/2.
\]
Its additional mass is at most
\[
 Q\min(h/w,1)\le\frac Q{\sqrt w}\sqrt h
 =qN^{-1/16}\sqrt h\le\sqrt h/2,
\]
where \(N^{1/16}\ge\sqrt2\). Thus \(\mu_N(J)\le\sqrt h\).

Next suppose \(h\ge N/4\) and \(J\) intersects at most one end band. Put \(\theta=h/N\in[1/4,1]\). The mass is at most \(\rho h+Q\), whereas
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 B(h)-\rho h
 &=\sqrt N(\sqrt\theta-\theta)
   +\gamma N^{1/4}\left(\frac\theta2+\theta^{1/4}\right)\\
 &\ge\gamma N^{1/4}\left(\frac18+\frac1{\sqrt2}\right)
 >\frac{3\gamma}{4}N^{1/4}=Q.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The last strict comparison follows from \(1/\sqrt2>5/8\), equivalently \(32>25\).

Finally suppose \(J\) intersects both end bands. If \(h=N-\delta\), then \(0\le\delta\le2w\), and the omitted pieces lie entirely in the two end bands. Their common density is \(D=\rho+Q/w\), so
\[
 \mu_N(J)=B(N)-D\delta.
\]
We have \(D\ge N^{-1/2}\), since
\[
 \frac Qw=qN^{-3/8}\ge\frac{\gamma}{2}N^{-3/4}.
\]
For every \(u\in[N-2w,N]\subseteq[N/2,N]\), using \(\gamma<1\) and \(N^{1/4}\ge4\),
\[
 \sqrt N\,B'(u)
 \le\frac1{\sqrt2}
       +\frac{\gamma2^{3/4}}{4N^{1/4}}
 <\frac34+\frac18=\frac78<1.
\]
It follows that
\[
 B(N)-B(h)\le\delta/\sqrt N\le D\delta,
\]
proving (E11) in the final case.

**Integer realization.** Let \(F(t)=\mu_N([0,t])\), let \(k_N=\lfloor B(N)\rfloor\), and choose the unique points \(x_j\in(0,N)\) satisfying
\[
 F(x_j)=j-\frac12,\qquad1\le j\le k_N.
\]
Existence and uniqueness follow because \(F\) is continuous and strictly increasing. Its density is everywhere at most
\[
 \rho+Q/w<N^{-1/2}+N^{-3/8}
 \le\frac1{16}+\frac18=\frac3{16}<1.
\]
Thus consecutive \(x_j\)'s are more than one unit apart. Define
\[
 B_N=\{\lceil x_j\rceil:1\le j\le k_N\}.
\]
These are distinct integers in \(\{1,\ldots,N\}\), proving (E7).

For \(I=\{a+1,\ldots,a+H\}\), membership \(\lceil x_j\rceil\in I\) is equivalent to \(a<x_j\le a+H\). The half-integer levels \(j-1/2\) are spaced by one, so their number in \((F(a),F(a+H)]\) is at most
\[
 F(a+H)-F(a)+1\le B(H)+1.
\]
This proves (E8) for every integer subinterval, simultaneously.

**Proof that it is not Sidon, including the small endpoint \(N=256\).**
The background density satisfies
\[
 \rho\ge\frac7{8\sqrt N},
\]
and \(N-2w\ge3N/4\). Hence the mass of the central interval \([w,N-w]\) is at least
\[
 \rho(N-2w)\ge\frac{21}{32}\sqrt N\ge\frac{21}{2}. \tag{E12}
\]
Moreover \(F(w)=\rho w+Q\ge Q\ge2\sqrt2>3/2\). By symmetry,
\[
 F(N-w)=B(N)-F(w)<B(N)-3/2<k_N-1/2.
\]
Thus the entire interval of mass levels \([F(w),F(N-w)]\) lies strictly between the first and last allowed half-integer levels. An interval of length at least \(21/2\) contains at least ten points of the lattice \(\mathbb Z+1/2\). There are therefore at least ten consecutive chosen quantile indices whose points lie in the central interval.

On that interval \(F(t)=\rho t+Q\), so those quantile points form an arithmetic progression with common difference \(1/\rho\). After taking ceilings, every consecutive gap belongs to
\[
 \{\lfloor1/\rho\rfloor,\ \lceil1/\rho\rceil\}.
\]
Among at least nine consecutive positive gaps, two have the same value. They give distinct representations of a positive difference. Consequently \(B_N\) is not strong Sidon for every \(N\ge256\). \(\square\)

This is a countermodel to the listed relaxation, not a counterexample to a Sidon theorem. Since
\[
 |B_N|=\sqrt N+\gamma N^{1/4}+O(1),
\]
any deduction based solely on (E8), actual interval-count realizability, and identities valid for arbitrary integer point sets cannot imply a uniform coefficient below \(\gamma\). The Riemann-sum error for the piecewise polynomial limiting integrand is \(O_s(x^{-1})\); the larger displayed error comes from (E13).

 b_{i+r}-b_i\le r/\rho+1.

 \rho M=\ell+\frac{\gamma s-s^2}{2}+O_s(x^{-1}). \tag{E15}

 R_\ell=O_s(x^2),\qquad
 C_\ell^*=O_s(x^4)=O_s(N)=o(N^{5/4}). \tag{E16}
\]
This estimate includes the binomial correction.

Consequently,
\[
\sum_{d\in\mathcal F_m}d=\frac{m(m+1)}2D-B_m,
\qquad
\boxed{M(M+1)+2B_m\le m(m+1)D.} \tag{H1}
\]
The inequality compares \(M\) distinct positive integers with \(1,\ldots,M\).
The identity remains valid if the two endpoint ranges overlap; no
disjointness hypothesis is used in this telescoping calculation.

This retains endpoint and index information discarded when a scalar kernel
bound simply adds every missing difference. It is not an occupancy moment.

A concrete sufficient improvement condition can be read from (H1). At
\(m\sim tN^{1/4}\), a uniform
\[
B_m\ge(\beta-o(1))m^3\sqrt N
\]
for the potentially extremal sets would imply
\[
2c\le t^{-1}+(1-2\beta)t.
\]
Thus \(\beta>1/18\) would beat \(2\sqrt2/3\). For example, the **unproved**
condition \(\beta=1/16\), at \(t=\sqrt{8/7}\), would give
\(c\le\sqrt{7/8}<2\sqrt2/3\). This is a conditional reduction, not a new bound.

Elementary counting in the first and last \(j\) points gives only
\[
E_j\ge j(j-1),\qquad
B_m\ge\frac{m(m+1)(m+2)(m-1)}{12}. \tag{H2}
\]
Each individual endpoint span is at least \(\binom j2\), because it contains
that many distinct positive differences. Summation yields the displayed
polynomial. For \(m\asymp N^{1/4}\), (H2) is of order \(N\), whereas the
sufficient condition needs order \(N^{5/4}\).

### H2. Reserved endpoint differences

Choose \(J\) with \(2J\le k\). Inside the first \(J\) points and inside the
last \(J\) points, reserve the differences with index separation greater
than \(m\). Their set \(\mathcal S\) is disjoint from \(\mathcal F_m\), and
\[
q=|\mathcal S|=(J-m)_+(J-m-1)_+,\qquad
W=\sum_{d\in\mathcal S}d.
\]
There are \(q/2\) reserved pairs at each endpoint. The two endpoint blocks
are disjoint, and global difference uniqueness prevents repetitions or
collisions with the selected family. Comparing their union with the first
\(M+q\) positive integers gives
\[
\boxed{(M+q)(M+q+1)+2B_m-2W\le m(m+1)D.} \tag{H3}
\]
Also,
\[
\boxed{W\le\frac q2 E_J.} \tag{H4}
\]
The latter uses the actual left and right spans separately, rather than
replacing their sum by twice their maximum.

This is the additional finite reduction: endpoint differences excluded from
the index band still occupy integer values and can raise the minimum sum of
the selected differences.

### H3. Fixed-parameter dichotomy, with uniform floor errors

Fix real parameters \(0<t<u<s\) and \(v\ge0\). Write
\[
x=N^{1/4},\quad c_N=(k-x^2)/x,\quad
m=\lfloor tx\rfloor,\quad J=\lfloor ux\rfloor,\quad n=\lfloor sx\rfloor.
\]
Only \(k>x^2\) needs consideration for the desired positive second-order
coefficient. Uniformly for these sets, \(0<c_N\le2\) once \(x\ge2\).
Indeed, apply (H1) with \(m_0=\lfloor x\rfloor\) and \(B_{m_0}\ge0\):
\[
k\le\frac{m_0+1}{2}+x^2\sqrt{1+1/m_0}
\le x^2+\frac32x+\frac12\le x^2+2x.
\]

Set
\[
X_0=\max\left\{2,\frac2t,\frac3u,\frac2{u-t},
                    \frac2{s-u},2s+2\right\}.
\]
For \(x\ge X_0\), all bands and endpoint blocks used below are valid.
In particular \(2J\le k\), \(m<J<n<k\), \(J\ge2\), and \(m\ge tx/2\).

For any fixed bandwidth parameter \(a>0\), assume \(x\ge\max\{2,2/a\}\) and \(1\le\lfloor ax\rfloor<k\), and define
\[
C_a=2/a^2+2a+4.
\]
The displayed onset \(X_0\) ensures these conditions for both parameters \(a=t\) and \(a=s\) used below.
Expanding (H1), dropping only terms in the safe direction, and putting
\(r=\lfloor ax\rfloor\) gives the uniform estimate
\[
2c_N\le a+a^{-1}
-\frac{2B_r}{r^2x^3}+\frac{C_a}{x}. \tag{H5}
\]
For completeness, before the final estimates its right side is bounded by
\[
\frac{x}{r}+\frac{(r+1)k}{x^3}+\frac1{x^3}
-\frac{2B_r}{r^2x^3}.
\]
Use \(x/r\le a^{-1}+2/(a^2x)\), \(k\le x^2+2x\), and \(r\le ax\).

**Small endpoint span.** If \(E_J\le vx^3\), (H3)--(H4) imply
\[
2c_N\le S(t,u,v)+\frac{K_S}{x},\qquad
S(t,u,v)=t+\frac1t-\frac{(u-t)^2(2t-v)}{t^2}. \tag{H6}
\]
Here an explicit sufficient error constant is \(K_S=C_t+H\), where, with
\(d=u-t\),
\[
A_t=t^2+5t+2,\qquad K=2t+v+A_t,
\]
\[
H=\frac{4(3d+2)K}{t^2}
  +\frac{4d^2A_t}{t^2}
  +\frac{8d^2|2t-v|}{t^3}.
\]
To verify uniformity, put \(p=m/x\), \(Q=q/x^2\), and \(V=2M/x^3\). Then
\[
|Q-d^2|\le(3d+2)/x,\quad |V-2t|\le A_t/x,\quad
p\ge t/2,\quad |p^{-2}-t^{-2}|\le8/(t^3x).
\]
Indeed, \(|(J-m)-dx|\le1\), \(q=(J-m)(J-m-1)\), and
\[
V=2p(1+c_N/x)-p^2/x-p/x^2.
\]
These bounds give
\[
\left|\frac{Q(V-v)}{p^2}
-\frac{d^2(2t-v)}{t^2}\right|\le H/x.
\]
This is the normalized reserved-difference correction; it proves (H6)
without any nonuniform sequence-dependent error.

**Large endpoint span.** If \(E_J\ge vx^3\), monotonicity gives
\[
B_n\ge\frac{(n-J+1)(n-J+2)}2 E_J.
\]
The floor factors satisfy
\((n-J+1)(n-J+2)\ge(s-u)^2x^2\), while \(n\le sx\). Thus
\[
2c_N\le L(s,u,v)+\frac{C_s}{x},\qquad
L(s,u,v)=s+\frac1s-v\frac{(s-u)^2}{s^2}. \tag{H7}
\]
There is no leading floor loss in this branch.

Consequently this family of arguments gives the uniform asymptotic
coefficient \(\frac12\max\{S,L\}\), with an explicit \(O_{t,u,s,v}(1)\)
cardinality remainder. The parameters are fixed as \(N\) varies. This
statement does not assert a uniform error when parameters vary with \(N\).

### H4. A restricted-family obstruction

For all \(0<t<u<s\) and \(v\ge0\),
\[
\boxed{\max\{S(t,u,v),L(s,u,v)\}
          \ge \frac{27}{10\sqrt2}.} \tag{H8}
\]
Hence this single-threshold, two-bandwidth dichotomy cannot certify a
second-order coefficient below
\[
\frac{27}{20\sqrt2}
=\frac{2\sqrt2}{3}+\frac1{60\sqrt2}.
\]
This is a limitation of (H6)--(H7), not a lower bound on actual Sidon
cardinalities, and not an obstruction to other ordered-difference arguments.

Here is a full proof. Put \(u_0=1/\sqrt2\) and
\(C_0=27/(10\sqrt2)<23/12<2\), and suppose \(S<C_0\).

First \(u>u_0\). If \(u\le u_0\), then
\[
S\ge S(t,u,0)=4u-t+\frac{1-2u^2}{t}
\ge u+\frac1u\ge2,
\]
since the middle expression decreases with \(t\) on \(0<t<u\).

Suppose next that \(u\le1\). The condition \(S<2\) implies \(v<t<u\):
with \(d=u-t\), it gives
\(d^2(2t-v)>t(t-1)^2\), while \((1-t)^2\ge d^2\).
It follows that
\[
L\ge G(s,u):=s+s^{-1}-u(1-u/s)^2.
\]
In fact \(G(s,u)\ge C_0\) for every \(u\ge u_0\), \(s\ge u\).
If \(s\ge3u_0\), the maximum of \(u(1-u/s)^2\) on \(0\le u\le s\)
is \(4s/27\), so
\[
G(s,u)\ge23s/27+s^{-1}\ge29/(9\sqrt2)>C_0.
\]
The last function increases on \(s\ge3u_0\). If instead
\(u_0\le s\le3u_0\), the subtracted term decreases on \(u_0\le u\le s\).
Writing \(z=s/u_0\in[1,3]\), one obtains
\[
G(s,u)\ge u_0(z-1+4/z-1/z^2)\ge C_0.
\]
For the final inequality multiply by \(10z^2\). The resulting polynomial
\[
P(z)=10z^3-37z^2+40z-10
\]
has derivative \(2(5z-4)(3z-5)\) and minimum \(P(5/3)=5/27>0\) on
\([1,3]\).

It remains to treat \(u\ge1\). The condition \(S<C_0<23/12\) gives
\[
v<2t-\frac{t(t-1)^2+t^2/12}{(u-t)^2}\le2u-1.
\]
For the second inequality, let \(d=u-t\). If \(d\ge1/2\), it follows
already from \(2t\le2u-1\). Otherwise \(t>1/2\), and
\[
t(t-1)^2+t^2/12>1/27\ge d^2(1-2d).
\]
For \(t\ge3/4\), the left side is at least \(3/64>1/27\).
For \(1/2<t<3/4\), it is at least \(1/32+1/48=5/96>1/27\).
The maximum of \(d^2(1-2d)\) on \([0,1/2]\) is \(1/27\).
Therefore
\[
L\ge s+s^{-1}-(2u-1)(1-u/s)^2\ge2.
\]
To verify the last step, multiply the difference from 2 by \(s^2\)
and put \(r=s-u\ge0\). It equals
\[
r^3+(u-1)r^2+(u-1)(3u-1)r+u(u-1)^2\ge0.
\]
Thus \(L\ge C_0\) whenever \(S<C_0\), proving (H8).

## Extra seed 9: overlapping multi-step gaps under an explicit rank-width hypothesis

INFORMED — OPEN for the original target. Worker A derived the restricted theorem below, after earlier Sidon/sonar proof exposure and without web, other attack reports or solvers. The root independently checked the derivation. The additional hypothesis is not known for arbitrary Sidon sets and is not treated as a hit on task038.

Empty sets satisfy the asserted size bounds directly; no range is defined for an empty sequence. Let the nonempty set A={a_0<...<a_(k−1)}⊆{1,...,N} be strong Sidon. Suppose an integer q≥1 satisfies

range(a_i−qi)≤q−1.                                                     (R0)

Then

N≥k²−2^(1/4)k^(3/2)−3k.                                               (R1)

Consequently, for every N≥120⁴ and every such set satisfying R0,

k≤√N+2^(−3/4)N^(1/4)+4.                                               (R2)

The coefficient obeys (2^(−3/4))²=2^(−3/2)<8/9. Both statements retain R0; neither is a bound for general Sidon sets.

To prove them, put b_i=a_i−qi and W=max b_i−min b_i≤q−1. For 1≤r<k let Δ_i^(r)=b_(i+r)−b_i and P_r=#{i:Δ_i^(r)>0}. For each r the increments are distinct integers, since equality repeats the positive difference a_(i+r)−a_i. Let F(t) count all positive differences of A not exceeding t. Because W<q, index gaps below r have physical difference less than rq, while index gaps above r have physical difference greater than rq. At index gap r, precisely the nonpositive increments contribute. Thus

F(rq)=rk−r(r+1)/2−P_r≤rq,
P_r≥r(k−q)−r(r+1)/2.                                                 (R3)

The shared gaps also telescope:

Σ_(i=0)^(k−r−1) Δ_i^(r)
 =Σ_(i=k−r)^(k−1)b_i−Σ_(i=0)^(r−1)b_i,
|Σ_i Δ_i^(r)|≤rW.                                                     (R4)

This retains overlap information lost if the difference labels are independently selected.

Cases k≤2 satisfy R1 directly. For k≥3 the endpoints give

N≥a_(k−1)−a_0+1≥q(k−2)+2.

If q≥k this already proves R1. Otherwise s=k−q is an integer with 1≤s<k. At r=s there are q increments and R3 gives P=P_s≥p_0=s(s−1)/2. Their positive sum is at least P(P+1)/2. At most q−P increments are negative. Their absolute values are distinct and at most W≤q−1, so their sum is at most the deliberately looser bound (q−P)(q+P+1)/2. Use the upper half of R4 to obtain

2P(P+1)≤q(q+1)+2sq=k²−s²+q,
s⁴−2s³+5s²−2s≤2k²+2q.                                               (R5)

For integer s≥1,

(s−1/2)⁴≤s⁴−2s³+5s²−2s.

Since q≤k, and (1+t)^(1/4)≤1+t/4 for t≥0,

s−1/2≤(2k²+2k)^(1/4)
 ≤2^(1/4)√k+2^(1/4)/(4√k)
 ≤2^(1/4)√k+1/2.

Hence s≤2^(1/4)√k+1. Substitution into N≥(k−s)(k−2)+2 proves R1, with positive terms safely discarded.

For the explicit inversion let x=N^(1/4)≥120 and c_0=2^(1/4)<3/2. The established general theorem gives k≤x²+(2√2/3)x+1≤(x+1)². Therefore R1 implies

k²≤x⁴+c_0(x+1)³+3(x+1)²≤x⁴+c_0x³+8x².

For the final inequality, the remaining coefficient after division by x² is at most

3c_0+3+(3c_0+6)/x+(c_0+3)/x²
 <15/2+21/(2·120)+9/(2·120²)<8.

The square of x²+(c_0/2)x+4 dominates x⁴+c_0x³+8x², proving R2 uniformly. This uses the earlier general bound solely for a coarse inversion, without assuming an improved coefficient.

The obstruction to global use is precise: R0 restricts detrended rank discrepancy to one spacing q. A scalar capacity profile may put order N^(1/4) points in an endpoint cluster of width order √N. With q of order √N, the corresponding qi values vary by order N^(3/4), much more than the cluster's physical width. This discussion does not construct an extremal Sidon set, but explains why the scalar profile does not by itself imply R0. No argument here proves that failure of the uniform energy gap (G) forces R0.
