## 6. Analytical routes ruled out in full

**Affine squares over Z/22Z.** A Latin affine square a*i+b*j+c requires both a and b to be units, hence odd. Translating both indices by 11 changes the value by 11(a+b), which is zero modulo 22. Thus any two such squares have the same ordered pair at (i,j) and (i+11,j+11), which are different cells. No two members of this entire affine family are orthogonal. As a small finite check, all 10,000 ordered quadruples of unit coefficients (a,b,c,d) were enumerated; every determinant a*d-b*c was even. Additive constants do not change this obstruction. This does not cover arbitrary isotopes using unrelated permutations of row and column labels.

**Direct product using orders 2 and 11.** Every order-2 Latin square is determined by the parity of its row plus column, up to interchanging the two symbols. Two such squares give only two different ordered symbol pairs, so order 2 cannot provide even two MOLS. The direct-product construction that asks for four MOLS in each factor therefore cannot reach four at order 22. No assertion is made about more general non-product constructions.

**Proper pairwise balanced designs with block sizes at least 5.** Suppose every pair of 22 points lies in exactly one block, every block is a proper subset, and every block has at least 5 points. Choose a block B of size b and a point x outside B. The b blocks joining x to the b points of B must be distinct: otherwise a pair of points in B would lie in two blocks. Any two of these blocks meet only at x, again by uniqueness of pairs. Each contains at least four other points, so 21 >= 4b. Thus b <= 5, since b is an integer. Every block consequently has size exactly 5. But the blocks through any point partition its 21 neighbours into sets of size 4, requiring 4 to divide 21, a contradiction. This rules out the entire stated PBD space, without computation. A block equal to all 22 points would merely assume the original target and is explicitly excluded.

**A precise version of the special difference-matrix hint.** A sufficient condition is a 44 by 6 array D over Z/22Z such that, for every pair of columns a,b and each difference d, exactly two rows have D[r,a]-D[r,b]=d, and the two corresponding entries D[r,a] have opposite parities. Develop each row by adding 2t to every coordinate, for t=0,...,10. For a desired ordered pair (x,y), its difference selects the two seed rows, parity of x selects one, and the first coordinate determines exactly one even translation. Hence the developed 484 rows form OA(6,22). Conversely an OA with this simultaneous +2 action yields such a seed by taking one representative from each of its 44 row orbits. This is a self-derived precise sufficient/necessary criterion for that action, not a claim about the meaning intended in the unspecified published remark.

A tempting subclass pairs every seed row u with u+(11,...,11). It is impossible already for three columns. Picking one row from each pair would yield a 22-row index-one difference matrix. Subtract its first column from every row. The next two columns would each be permutations of Z/22Z, so both have sum 0+...+21 = 231 = 11 modulo 22.
