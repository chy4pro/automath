## N4. The elementary ceiling, with the normalization fixed

Write

    S(u,v;p) = sum_{x in F_p^*} e_p(ux+v/x),
    Kl_2(t;p) = p^(-1/2) S(t,1;p).

Weil gives |S(u,v;p)| <= 2 sqrt(p) when uv is nonzero. If exactly one argument is zero the sum is -1. At (0,0) it is p-1. These exceptional frequencies must be separated before using a square-root entry bound.

Here are direct deductions, not claims of optimality.

1. For an interval J of length N and b nonzero, completing sum_{x in J, x!=0} e_p(ax+b/x) gives O(sqrt(p) log p). Relative to the trivial N bound, this is nontrivial for N > sqrt(p) log p, hence gives a power saving when N=p^theta with fixed theta>1/2.
2. For arbitrary coefficient sequences supported on intervals of lengths M,L, the complete-Kloosterman bilinear form B=sum alpha_m beta_n Kl_2(amn;p) has the elementary bounds O(||alpha||_2 ||beta||_2 sqrt(ML)) and O(||alpha||_2 ||beta||_2 sqrt(p)). Opening the Kloosterman sum, applying Cauchy–Schwarz and Parseval proves the second. On equal lengths L=p^lambda it improves the entrywise bound only when lambda>1/2.
3. To see the R3 ceiling, let U f(x)=f(x^(-1)), with 0^(-1)=0, and use the unitary additive Fourier transform. For nonzero frequencies its matrix is

       Uhat(xi,eta) = [S(-xi,eta;p)+1]/p.

   The +1 makes the convention exact. U fixes constants and is unitary; the uncorrected S/p matrix is the transform of the inversion operator with the value at zero deleted. Thus referring to Kl_2 itself as a unitary matrix would miss a factor sqrt(p), as well as the zero-point convention.

   For frequency sets of size X, entrywise Weil and the Hilbert–Schmidt bound give

       ||P_X Uhat P_X|| <= min(1, (2+p^(-1/2)) X/sqrt(p)).

   At the ideal smoothing scale X approximately p/N, the useful factor is sqrt(p)/N. Its exponents at the two requested scales are 7/18 and 22/45, both positive. After taking the minimum with 1, this certifies no cancellation.

This is an exact block calculation. Indicators of disjoint interval unions are not exactly band limited; applying it to them also needs a smoothing/tail estimate. That additional work cannot make this particular entrywise bound give a saving below theta=1/2. Conversely, the calculation does not rule out exploiting correlations between entries or structure of the coefficients.

A different normalization illustrates why the qualification matters: completing the count xy=1 inside one N by N rectangle gives main term N^2/p and error O(sqrt(p) log^2 p). An asymptotic from that bound requires N>p^(3/4) up to logarithms. The 1/2 and 3/4 thresholds answer different questions.

## N4. Exact source bounds and their applicable lengths

All savings below are asymptotic power savings; factors p^epsilon or p^o(1) remain. These are not bounds with computed numerical leading coefficients or computed starting primes. “Type I” means one coefficient sequence is an interval indicator or has the required smoothness; Type II permits both sequences to be arbitrary.

### Complete Kloosterman sums, Type II

**Kowalski–Michel–Sawin 2017.** [Theorem 1.1 and Remark 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.01636), for the normalized Kl_k kernel, give

    |B| <<_epsilon p^epsilon ||alpha||_2 ||beta||_2 (ML)^(1/2)
             [M^(-1/2)+(ML)^(-3/16) p^(11/64)].

Here alpha is supported on [1,M], beta on an interval of length L, 1<=M<=L p^(1/4), and p^(1/4)<ML<p^(5/4). On M=L=p^lambda, the saving against the entrywise bound is min(lambda/2,3lambda/8-11/64), positive for lambda>11/24; at lambda=1/2 it is 1/64. For the inversion block, division by sqrt(p) instead requires lambda<21/40 for the second term to be o(1). Neither long target block lies in this useful range.

**Kowalski–Michel–Sawin 2020, updated arXiv version v5.** [Theorems 1.2 and 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.09849) give, for trivial twists and every integer ell>=2,

    |B| << p^epsilon ||alpha||_2 ||beta||_2 (ML)^(1/2)
       [1/M + (p^(3/4+3/(4ell))/(ML))^(1/ell)]^(1/2),

provided either p^(3/(2ell))<=L<(1/2)p^(1/2+3/(4ell)), or p^(3/(2ell))<=L and L Mplus<(1/2)p^(1+3/(2ell)), where Mplus is the largest integer in alpha's support. In the balanced initial-interval case, the displayed saving is

    min(lambda/2, (2lambda-3/4-3/(4ell))/(2ell)).

Taking ell sufficiently large reaches every lambda>3/8. The range conditions and the saving relative to sqrt(p), rather than relative to L, must still be checked for R3. They do not give an improvement for its long dual blocks.

**Blomer–Pascadi 2026.** [Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24311v1#Thmtheorem1.1), for intervals of length at most L and arbitrary coefficients, gives the unnormalized S form

    |sum alpha_m beta_n S(am,n;c)|
      << ||alpha||_2 ||beta||_2 c^(1+o(1))
         [L^(1/8)c^(-3/32)+L^(5/16)c^(-3/16)+L^(2/3)c^(-7/18)].

The summation requires (m,n,c)=1; initial intervals allow its removal. At L=sqrt(c) the saving is c^(-1/32). The formula beats both elementary bounds when 13/28<log_c L<7/12, away from endpoints. The paper identifies KMS's 3/8 as the smaller prime-modulus Type-II length threshold. For c=p, the inversion-block bound is exactly the bracket above, up to p^o(1) and the rank-one correction.

Substituting L=p^(1-theta) is our calculation. All three terms are o(1) precisely when theta>5/12. At theta=1/9 their exponents are 5/288,13/144,11/54; at theta=1/90 they are 43/1440,35/288,73/270. Thus this direct application does not improve norm 1 at either target. The new analytic result improves the ideal physical-scale ceiling to 5/12, not to 1/9.

**Pascadi 2025/2026.** [Theorems 1.1, 1.2, 7.8 and Example 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08445v2) distinguish factorable composite moduli from primes. At lengths at most c^(1/2+o(1)), Theorem 1.1 gives ||alpha||||beta|| c^(1-1/700+o(1)). If c=dd'e, d' divides d, (d,e)=1, and f is the largest integer with f^2 dividing cd, Theorem 1.2 replaces the saving by [f/min(c,d^2)]^(1/6). For c a product of two comparable distinct primes, this is c^(-1/12); it is not a prime-modulus saving. At equal lengths L=c^lambda, Theorem 7.8(i)'s relative exponents are

    (11+53delta)/64-3lambda/8,
    (1-delta)/6-lambda/3,
    (4-delta)/12-2lambda/3,
    11/24-lambda,                  0<=delta<=1/24.

Optimizing for positivity of the savings gives lambda>20/41=1/2-1/82. These results neither justify N=p^(1/9) as a short complete-Kloosterman length nor give the required long prime-block estimate. Blomer–Pascadi improves the general-modulus bound near sqrt(c).

**Shkredov's existing expansion bound.** [Theorem 4 of arXiv:1905.00291](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.00291) gives

    |sum alpha_m beta_n S(m,n;p)|
       << ||alpha||_2 ||beta||_2 p^(1-delta(c)),

up to logarithms, for interval supports whenever one length is at most p^(1-c), c>0. The positive delta(c) is not numerically specified in the theorem. Hence both long target blocks are covered qualitatively by taking c=1/9 or 1/90. Its Type-II argument uses group expansion; Pascadi's introduction explicitly identifies the flattening dependence. It supplies no verified new numerical kappa for the campaign. It is incorrect to state that no published bilinear cancellation covers the long-block regime.

### Short phases and Type I results: do not identify them with R3's kernel

The task's “Shkredov–Shparlinski” label is not an identified joint theorem on arbitrary-coefficient complete-Kloosterman blocks. The source-checked relevant statements are Shkredov's theorem above and the Shparlinski-school results below.

**Kerr–Shparlinski–Wu–Xi.** [Theorems 2.1 and 2.4, v5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.05038) give, when the modulus and a are coprime,

    |sum_{m in I,n in J} alpha_m S(m,an;p)|
      << ||alpha||_2 sqrt(M) L sqrt(p) p^o(1)
         [M^(-1/4)L^(-1)p^(1/2)+p^(1/2)L^(-1)M^(-1/2)+L^(-1/2)].

For equal lengths the leading saving requires lambda>2/5, and is 1/8 at lambda=1/2. This is Type I. Separately, for W=sum_{m in I,1<=k<=L} alpha_m gamma_k e_p(am/k), their (2.2a) gives

    |W| << ||alpha||_2 ||gamma||_infinity sqrt(M)L p^o(1)
                   [M^(-1/4)+M^(-1/2)+L^(-1/2)].

For bounded coefficients and M=L=p^theta, it saves theta/4: 1/36 at 1/9 and 1/360 at 1/90. This is a bilinear inverse phase, not a bilinear sum of complete Kloosterman sums.

**Bourgain–Garaev.** [Theorems 7–10 of arXiv:1211.4184](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.4184) concern W=sum alpha_m beta_n e_p(a/(mn)), with bounded coefficients. For initial intervals, Theorem 8 gives

    |W|/(ML) <<_{k1,k2} (log p)^(2(k1/k2+k2/k1))
      [(M^(k1-1)/sqrt(p)+sqrt(p)/M^k1)
       (L^(k2-1)/sqrt(p)+sqrt(p)/L^k2)]^(1/(2k1k2)).

The printed constant is (2k1)^(45 k1^2 k2)(2k2)^(45 k2^2 k1). With M=L=p^(1/9), k1=k2=5 yields p^(-1/450) times (log p)^4 and that fixed constant. At exactly p^(1/90), k=45 or46 is resonant and this formula yields no power saving. Theorem 7 instead permits unequal shifted intervals longer than p^(1/18) and p^(5/12+epsilon). Theorem 9 gives p^(1/8)(ML)^(3/4)[(1+M^3/p)(1+L^3/p)]^(1/16); at short equal lengths it needs theta>1/4. None of these statements substitutes its phase for Kl_2.

Consequently, neither requested scale is covered by the inspected short-length complete-Kloosterman Type-II formulas. Some differently normalized phase sums are nontrivial at these scales. R3 must establish a reduction that preserves the kernel, arbitrary-coefficient norms, and the p factors before importing such a saving.

## N5. Explicit SL2 growth

The strongest general symmetric-set exponent I located and read is **1/20**, in [Rudnev–Shkredov, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.01671) (journal: Mathematika 68 (2022), 738–783). If A is a symmetric generating subset of SL2(F_p), and |A| exceeds an absolute constant, then either A^3=SL2(F_p) or |A^3|/|A| >> |A|^(1/20). The displayed coefficient and onset are not computed. The paper discusses 1/15 conditionally on an appropriate SL2 direction theorem, and an “as if 1/12” phenomenon for diameter; neither is a general 1/15 or 1/12 tripling theorem. Its large-set subcases are also not replacements over the entire flattening range.

[Kowalski, Theorem 1.2 and equation (3.14)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1201.1139) distinguish an all-size exponent 1/3024 from a bound with coefficient 2^(-1/2) and exponent 1/1512 (p>7, unless the triple product is the whole group). Both are weaker. The paper's introductory 1/1513 for sufficiently large sets absorbs the coefficient and is not an improvement over 1/20.

The targeted author/name searches found no stronger general SL2 exponent. Murphy–Petridis's [Products of Differences over Arbitrary Finite Fields](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.06581) concerns additive/multiplicative expansion in fields, not this SL2 tripling statement. The affine-group literature also does not transfer merely because an exponent is larger. I did not locate a specific Murphy–Petridis theorem claiming otherwise. This is a bounded literature-search conclusion, not a proof of universal absence.

### A fully traceable, deliberately conservative fallback

The following derivation is included to make the audit constructive. It is not asserted as the best published pair, and it is worse than the campaign baseline. Use the energy convention required by Tao: for E(A,A^(-1)) apply his theorem to the equal-size pair (A,A^(-1)); for E(A,A) use (A,A). Put |A|=n. In either case D=A' is contained in A, |D| >> n/K, and there is B' of size >>n/K with |DB'| << K^8 n.

The noncommutative Ruzsa triangle inequality gives

    |DD^(-1)| <= |DB'|^2/|B'| << K^17 n << K^18 |D|.

Take L=C K^18 for a sufficiently large absolute C and the popular-shift S above. Write m=|D| and r(s)=|D intersect Ds|. We have sum r=m^2, r<=m, and

    sum r(s)^2 = E(D^(-1),D)=E(D,D^(-1)) >= m^4/|DD^(-1)| >= m^3/L.

The contribution of s outside S is at most m^3/(2L). Therefore sum_{s in S}r(s)>=m^2/(2L). Averaging over d in D gives a d for which

    T = S intersect d^(-1)D,
    |T| >= m/(2L).

Since T subset S and T subset d^(-1)D,

    T^3 subset d^(-1) D S^2,
    |T^3| <= |DS^2D^(-1)| <= 4L^5m <= 8L^6|T|.

Thus **(C1,C2)=(19,108)** is a proved consequence of the source-checked inputs, with unspecified absolute coefficients, for a translated subset T. T need not be symmetric; applying the symmetric growth theorem still needs the C6 repair. This fallback alone does not validate the whole flattening chain.

The G0 worker independently inspected the Shkredov TeX and reported comments suggesting the input |D| approximately n/K, |DD^(-1)| approximately K^7 n and the arithmetic (2*1+1)(7+1)+(7+1)=32. I have not independently downloaded that TeX. Even granting that stronger input, the derivation just given yields L approximately K^8 and **(9,48)**. The n=1 estimate controls T T T^(-1) through d^(-1)DSD^(-1)d, whereas T^3 requires n=2 in the displayed containment. Substituting a mixed triple product for a cube is not legitimate in a noncommutative group. This identifies a gap in this reconstruction, not a counterexample to the printed theorem. Both the stronger energy-to-doubling input and the exponent32 remain unverified.

### Other explicit constants and recent improvements

[Petridis, Theorems 1.6–1.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1101.3507) gives |B^h|<=alpha^(8h-17) beta^(h-2)|B| for h>2, assuming |BB|<=alpha|B| and |BbB|<=beta|B| for every b in B. Thus h=3 gives alpha^7 beta. The asymmetric version gives |SB^h|<=alpha^(8h-9)beta^(h-1)gamma^(4h-5)|S|, under |AB|<=alpha|A|, |AbB|<=beta|A| and |A|<=gamma|B|. These are valuable explicit product-set results, but the sandwich-product hypotheses are additional inputs; energy alone does not provide them for free. They do not furnish a validated replacement C1+C2 in this audit.

[Reiher–Schoen, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.10245v2) obtains |A'|>=(1-epsilon)K^(-1/2)|A| and |A'-A'|<=2^33 epsilon^(-9)K^4|A'|. Its ambient group is explicitly abelian. It cannot be inserted into SL2. The [2025 hypergraph BSG improvement](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06131v2) also explicitly says that its new proof does not generalize to noncommutative groups. No applicable 2020s smaller translated-cube pair was found. The “BSG for real numbers” item mentioned in N9 was not located/read; it is not an input here.

Avoiding BSG has two different meanings. Rudnev–Shkredov prove their set-growth theorem by geometric/dimensional and pivot arguments, so BSG is not needed inside that proof of tripling growth. Converting high energy or a large convolution norm into a set to which that theorem applies is the separate BSG step in the expansion machine. I found no source with a complete explicit replacement for that step over the campaign's full measure range. Pascadi and Blomer–Pascadi instead bypass flattening for particular bilinear forms; those do not state the R2 flattening lemma.
