## 1. Keep three different operators separate

Let X = P^1(F_p), p odd, with counting-measure inner product. Set

    h_j(x) = 1/(x+2j) - 2j,
    H_j = [[-2j, 1-4j^2], [1, 2j]],
    T_N = (1/N) sum_{j=1}^N P_{h_j}.

Direct multiplication gives H_j^2 = I and det H_j = -1. Consequently T_N is self-adjoint and preserves constants, and its mean-zero norm is its largest absolute nonconstant eigenvalue. Its entries count the relation in Lemma 14. These matrices naturally lie in the determinant-minus-one coset in GL_2, viewed projectively. Products H_i H_j lie in SL_2. For p congruent to 3 modulo 4, an individual H_j cannot be rescaled over F_p to determinant one. One can use the two-step law on SL_2, but must account for the squaring of eigenvalues. This is a normalization issue, not a counterexample to Shkredov's bound.

The two-parameter inversion operator is instead

    Q_N f(x) = (1/N^2) sum_{a,b=1}^N f(1/(x+a)-b).

It is a product of two translation averages and inversion. In general it is not self-adjoint. Its singular value is obtained from Q_N^* Q_N, not from a signed Rayleigh quotient for Q_N. The comparator 2 sqrt(N-1)/N belongs to the N-involution free-product model for T_N; there is no derivation making it a prediction for Q_N.

Finally, the R2 task concerns convolution on the entire group G = SL_2(F_p), with

    E(mu) = ||mu||_2^2 - 1/|G|,
    E(mu*mu) <= p^(-2c) E(mu).

This is stronger than controlling the single projective permutation representation. Even an excellent projective spectral estimate cannot simply be substituted as this full-group inequality. For example, the nonzero signed measure delta_I - delta_{-I} acts as zero on X. More generally the permutation representation sees only a small part of the regular representation. This kernel example explains the missing implication; it is not a probability-measure counterexample in the task's norm window.

## 2. Exact family setting an upper ceiling

Here is a complete elementary calculation; it also checks that a concentration argument must track actual mass rather than only support.

Let H be any proper subgroup, u_H its uniform probability, u_G the uniform probability on G, and 0 < w < 1. Define

    mu = (1-w)u_G + w u_H.

The measure is symmetric. Since u_H*u_H = u_H and convolution with u_G yields u_G,

    mu*mu = (1-w^2)u_G + w^2 u_H,
    E(mu) = w^2(1/|H| - 1/|G|),
    E(mu*mu)/E(mu) = w^2.                         (1)

For any coset xK of any proper subgroup K, put b_p = max_{K<G}|K|/|G|. Then

    mu(xK) <= (1-w)b_p + w.

In SL_2(F_p) for sufficiently large p, the subgroup classification gives b_p = 1/(p+1). Only b_p = O(1/p) is needed here. For a = tau/6 in (0,1), take w = p^(-a)/2. Since b_p = o(p^(-a)), all cosets satisfy the required cap p^(-a) for sufficiently large p.

Choose H to be the upper unipotent group, |H| = p. Then

    E(mu) = (1/4)p^(-1-2a)(1 - 1/(p^2-1)).        (2)

For any fixed h0 <= 1+2a and c satisfying 1+2a < 2-2c, (2) lies in the requested window for all sufficiently large p. But (1) is (1/4)p^(-2a), so for every c > a the claimed flattening inequality eventually fails. Therefore in this parameter range any universal lemma necessarily has

    c <= tau/6.

For tau = 1/4 this ceiling is 1/24; for tau = 1/5 it is 1/30. Both are above 1/468 and 1/120. This family therefore does NOT refute either campaign target. A torus of order p plus or minus one gives the same exponents. A Borel of order p(p-1) gives E(mu) of order p^(-2-2a), below the requested lower norm cutoff, so a Borel-mixture argument cannot be used to kill this window.

The subgroup-size fact used above is the standard classification input already assumed by the campaign, not an independent re-proof of Dickson's theorem in this report. Conditional only on b_p=O(1/p), all displayed computations and the stated ceiling are exact/asymptotic deductions with their onsets qualified.

## 3. What the printed asymmetric theorem actually gives

Murphy's Theorem 24 has alpha_J = 2(alpha/2)^(2^J) and K = (|Sym_{alpha_J}(Y)|/|A|)^(1/J), and extracts a set of tripling at most an unspecified constant times (K/alpha_J)^C. Use J>=1; the displayed definition divides by J despite the surrounding statement allowing J=0. Section 5.2 explicitly relates the argument to flattening. Its finite-field application still uses a product theorem and unspecified exponents. None of these statements prints a usable numerical c for this task. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.13569v1#S4)

The following parameter calculation is my deduction, not a numerical constant claimed by Murphy. If alpha = p^(-s) and a bound |Sym_{alpha_J}(Y)|/|A| <= p^b is the available information, then

    log_p(alpha_J^(-1)) = 2^J s + (2^J-1) log_p 2,
    log_p(K/alpha_J) <= b/J + 2^J s + (2^J-1)log_p 2.       (3)

Thus a bound of the stated shape has exponent C(b/J + 2^J s + o(1)), not Cb/J alone. Suppressing b/J by increasing J worsens the other term exponentially. Formula (3) is an accounting obstruction to the claimed bypass; it is not a lower bound on every possible new inverse theorem. Even recovering all hidden C values would still leave this tradeoff to optimize. Replacing tripling growth by its symmetric 1/20 version also requires checking the extracted set and symmetrization costs.

The abelian Pluennecke route has no justified application here: commutation and translation invariance on an abelian ambient group are unavailable for the Mobius family. Merely naming a Petridis improvement cannot supply a noncommutative weighted inverse statement. A pivot argument starts from small product sets; starting from high energy still requires a proved extraction/structure step with quantitative losses.

## 4. A concrete test of the new planar incidence input

Lewko v1 states I(P,L) = O((mn)^(2/3)+m+n+mn/p), with characteristic p rather than field cardinality. The end of its proof gives the explicit bound 3(mn)^(2/3)+2mn/p+2m+n. I read the polynomial construction and multiplicity argument but have not conducted an isolated adversarial verification of this recent preprint, and make every use below conditional on that displayed bound. Its sum-product corollary concerns sets of size at most p^(41/71), with exponent 52/41-epsilon. Neither statement is a centered Mobius mixing theorem. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.27023v1)

There is a simple exact reduction for the one-parameter family. Let A,B be subsets of F_p, let N<p, and put a=|A|, b=|B|. Map an ordered pair (x,y) to

    (s,t) = (x+y, xy).

Each image has at most two preimages, since x and y are the roots of Z^2-sZ+t. The equation (x+2j)(y+2j)=1 becomes the line incidence

    t + 2j s = 1 - 4j^2.

These N lines are distinct for odd p. Applying the displayed incidence bound and allowing the multiplicity two proves, conditionally on that bound,

    #{(x,y,j): (x+2j)(y+2j)=1}
      <= 6(abN)^(2/3) + 4abN/p + 4ab + 2N.                (4)

This is a derivation with all multiplicities included, not an identification of Mobius curves with lines in general. If a=b=m and N=p^theta, the term 4m^2 alone divided by the trivial mN bound is 4m/N. At campaign densities m>=p^(1-eta), this grows as p^(1-eta-theta). Both theta=1/9 and theta=1/90 are far below 1-eta. Moreover the main-density term in (4) has coefficient four, so subtracting abN/p does not produce a power-saving centered error. Thus this natural application of even the asserted sharp planar bound gives no useful dense-set cancellation.

The failure of (4) does not rule out a higher-moment construction using this incidence theorem. Such a construction would have to control weights, diagonals, the correct main term and new rational-curve families. It is the missing lemma, not a consequence already present in the source.

## 5. Three further non-implications

1. Fixed-generator comparison: if an average over r fixed generators has norm at most 1-epsilon and the remaining generators have norm at most one, convexity yields only ||T_N|| <= 1-r epsilon/N. Even partitioning into bounded-size conjugate blocks with a common gap gives a constant bound 1-epsilon. Neither is N^(-kappa) for a fixed kappa>0 as N tends to infinity. This rules out that comparison argument, not multiscale amplification.
2. Finite experiments: matching the free-product value at selected p,N does not certify a uniform all-prime norm bound or give the value of c in a group convolution lemma.
3. Squaring and centering: for a nonsymmetric measure one must work with reflected convolution to obtain a positive operator, and remove the uniform component before using a spectral gain. An action bound for the one-parameter family cannot be transferred to the independent two-parameter family by identifying their number of terms.
