## 1. Conventions and scope

Let `G = SL_2(F_p)`, with odd prime `p`, `N = |G| = p(p^2-1)`, `u = 1/N`, and `a = tau/6`. Counting measure defines every norm. Convolution is

`(f*g)(x) = sum_y f(y) g(y^{-1}x)`,

and `tilde f(x) = conjugate(f(x^{-1}))`. A symmetric probability measure is a nonnegative function of total mass one with `mu = tilde mu`. Write

`t(mu) = ||mu||_2^2 - 1/N = ||mu-u||_2^2`.

The target is

`t(mu*mu) <= p^{-2c} t(mu)`

under `mu(xH) <= p^{-a}` for **every** left coset of **every proper subgroup**, and

`p^{-2+2c} <= t(mu) <= p^{-h0}`.

Right cosets are covered as left cosets of conjugate subgroups. The parameter `h0` is left symbolic; the task does not supply a certified numerical value. Numerical examples below that use `h0=1/10` are conditional illustrations, not a certification of the initial stage.

For a finite set A put

`E(A) = ||1_A * 1_{A^{-1}}||_2^2 = #{(a,b,c,d) in A^4 : ab^{-1}=cd^{-1}}`,

`alpha(A) = max_{x,H<G} |A intersect xH|/|A|`,

`K_E(A) = |A|^3/E(A)`.

These are ordered quadruples; no diagonal terms are removed. When A is symmetric, `E(A)=||1_A*1_A||_2^2`. The unrelated parameter `K_T(A)=|A^3|/|A|` must not be identified with `K_E(A)`.

All proofs below are ordinary mathematical arguments, using the classical Frobenius lower bound `d_rho >= (p-1)/2` for nontrivial irreducible complex representations of G where stated. There has been no kernel formalization or independent referee verification.

## 2. A proved restricted theorem: conjugacy-invariant measures

**Proposition 1.** Suppose `p>=5` and mu is a conjugacy-invariant probability measure on G. Symmetry is not needed in this proposition. Let `Z={I,-I}`. Then

`t(mu*mu) <= [mu(Z) + 5 p^{-1/2}]^2 t(mu)`.

Consequently, if `mu(Z)<=p^{-a}`, `0<c<min(a,1/2)`, and

`p >= max{5, 2^{1/(a-c)}, 10^{1/(1/2-c)}}`,                         (2.1)

then the requested inequality holds, with coefficient one, throughout every norm range.

In particular, the original all-coset hypothesis implies `mu(Z)<=p^{-a}`. When `tau>=1/5`, one has `a>=1/30`, and `c=1/120` is valid for every prime `p>=2^40`: the first power in (2.1) is at most `2^40`, and the second is `10^{120/59}<2^40`.

**Proof.** Use Fourier transforms `mu_hat(rho)=sum_g mu(g)rho(g)` in unitary irreducible representations. Conjugacy invariance and Schur's lemma give

`mu_hat(rho) = z_rho I`, with `z_rho = sum_g mu(g) chi_rho(g)/d_rho`.

For a central element, `|chi_rho(g)|=d_rho`. For noncentral g, character-column orthogonality gives

`|chi_rho(g)|^2 <= sum_sigma |chi_sigma(g)|^2 = |C_G(g)|`.

The centralizer of a non-scalar two-by-two matrix is its two-dimensional commutant algebra. Intersecting its units with determinant one gives size `p-1` in the split semisimple case, `p+1` in the nonsplit case, and `2p` in the noncentral repeated-eigenvalue case. Thus `|C_G(g)|<=2p`. By Frobenius and `p>=3`, `d_rho >= (p-1)/2 >= p/3`. Hence, for nontrivial rho,

`|z_rho| <= mu(Z) + (sqrt(2p)/(p/3))(1-mu(Z))`

`             <= mu(Z) + 3 sqrt(2) p^{-1/2}`

`             <= mu(Z) + 5 p^{-1/2}`.

Plancherel now gives the exact identities

`t(mu) = N^{-1} sum_{rho != 1} d_rho^2 |z_rho|^2`,

`t(mu*mu) = N^{-1} sum_{rho != 1} d_rho^2 |z_rho|^4`.

Their ratio is bounded by `max |z_rho|^2`. Condition (2.1) makes each of `p^{-a}` and `5p^{-1/2}` at most `(1/2)p^{-c}`. This proves the assertions. QED.

The only representation-theoretic input specific to G is Frobenius's dimension bound; it is also an input in the energy/quasirandomness discussion of [Rudnev–Shkredov, §2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1812.01671v3#S2.SS3). Character-column orthogonality and Schur's lemma are standard finite-group identities.

**Why this is not an integration result.** Averaging an arbitrary measure over conjugation replaces each Fourier block by its scalar trace part. This can remove large eigenvalues; there is no reverse comparison supplied by the hypotheses. For example, `u_U` for a nontrivial unipotent subgroup U has nontrivial H-fixed Fourier vectors with eigenvalue one, whereas its conjugacy average has central mass `1/p` and is subject to the bound above. The example is used only to disprove a general reverse comparison, not as an admissible target measure. Neither the prescribed initial measure nor its subsequent convolutions have been proved conjugacy invariant.

## 3. Exact subgroup and coset tests

First, a useful bound requiring no subgroup classification: every proper subgroup H has

`[G:H] >= (p+1)/2`, hence `|H|/N <= 2/(p+1)`.

Indeed, the permutation representation on G/H has a nonzero complement to its constant vector, of dimension `[G:H]-1`; this complement contains a nontrivial irreducible representation, and Frobenius applies.

**Proposition 2.** Let `0<a<1`, let H be any proper subgroup of size m, and put

`w = (1/2)p^{-a}`, `mu = (1-w)u + w u_H`.

For sufficiently large p this is a symmetric admissible measure for the all-coset cap, and

`t(mu) = w^2(1/m - 1/N)`,

`t(mu*mu) = w^4(1/m - 1/N)`,

`t(mu*mu)/t(mu) = w^2 = (1/4)p^{-2a}`.                       (3.1)

**Proof.** On any proper coset C,

`mu(C) <= w + |C|/N <= (1/2)p^{-a} + 2/(p+1) <= p^{-a}`

once `p>=4^{1/(1-a)}`. The elementary identities `u*u_H=u` and `u_H*u_H=u_H` yield `mu*mu=(1-w^2)u+w^2u_H`. Taking centered squared norms proves (3.1). QED.

The family violates the proposed inequality whenever `c>a` and `p>2^{1/(c-a)}`, provided it lies in the task's norm range. Its relevance by subgroup type is as follows.

| H | Size m | Exponent of t(mu), up to a fixed coefficient | Norm-range implication |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Unipotent U | p | `1+2a` | Inside if `h0<1+2a<2-2c` |
| Split/non-split torus normalizer | `2(p-1)` / `2(p+1)` | `1+2a` | Same strict range condition |
| Borel B | `p(p-1)` | `2+2a` | Below the lower bound for every c>0 |
| Any fixed-order proper subgroup | fixed m | `2a` | Inside only when the upper norm condition permits it; e.g. `h0<2a` |

The unipotent example therefore gives the necessary ceiling `c<=a=tau/6` for the relevant parameter range around the requested targets. This is **not** a ceiling below `1/468` or `1/120`: at `tau=1/4` it is `1/24`, and at `tau=1/5` it is `1/30`.

If `h0>2a`, the fixed-order example can instead use `w=(1/2)p^{-b}` with `b>=max(a,h0/2)`, giving the analogous ceiling `c<=b` when its lower norm bound holds. This is weaker than the unipotent obstruction for the sample `h0=1/10`. It does not supply a new target counterexample. The calculation covers exceptional subgroups whenever they exist, without requiring a choice of primes for each exceptional isomorphism type.

For a nonsymmetric variant take `nu=u_H*delta_g` with `g in N_G(H)`. Then `nu*nu=u_H*delta_{g^2}` and replacing `u_H` by nu in the mixture leaves (3.1) unchanged. Taking H=U and an appropriate diagonal g in its normalizer makes the coset distinct from its inverse. Thus dropping symmetry does not by itself strengthen this obstruction.

**Near-subgroup test.** A measure assigning mass `1-o(1)` to one subgroup or one of its cosets violates the cap. Diluting that mass to `p^{-a}` yields the exact computation above. Calling such a diluted example a counterexample at `c=1/468` would reverse the inequality between c and a.

## 4. A proved obstruction to direct energy substitution in Larsen–Pink

The candidate shortcut tested here is

`|A intersect l| <= C K_E(A) |A|^{1/3}`                       (4.1)

for symmetric, nonconcentrated generating sets, where l is a unipotent coset line and C is absolute. Its tripling-parameter analogue is part of [Rudnev–Shkredov, Lemma 7](https://arxiv.org/html/1812.01671v3#S2.SS2). **Equation (4.1) is false**, including for uniform measures satisfying the task's cap and norm range.

**Proposition 3.** Fix `0<a<1/5`. For every sufficiently large prime p there is a symmetric set `A subset G`, containing the full standard unipotent subgroup U, such that

`4p^{1+a} <= |A| <= 13p^{1+a}`,

`alpha(A) <= (1/2)p^{-a}`,

`|A intersect U|=p`, and `K_E(A) <= 13^3 p^{3a}`.             (4.2)

In particular A generates G, and (4.1) fails for every fixed C as p tends to infinity.

**Construction and proof.** Partition `G\U` into inversion orbits, of sizes one or two. Select each orbit independently with probability

`r = 8p^{1+a}/(N-p)`

and let S be their union. For sufficiently large p, `0<r<1`, and `E|S|=8p^{1+a}`. Chernoff bounds for independent variables in `[0,2]` give

`P(|S| outside [4p^{1+a},12p^{1+a}]) <= 2 exp(-p^{1+a}/3)`.

For a fixed proper coset C, let Y_C count the selected inversion orbits meeting C. There are at most `|C|` eligible orbits and `|S intersect C|<=2Y_C`. Using the proper-coset size bound from §3,

`E Y_C <= r |C| <= 32 p^a`.

The usual binomial upper-tail estimate therefore gives, for large p,

`P(|S intersect C|>p) <= (64 e p^{a-1})^{p/2}`.

There are at most `N^{s+1}` subgroup cosets, where `s=floor(log_2 N)`. To see this without Dickson's classification, build a generating sequence for a subgroup by adjoining an element outside the subgroup already generated. Every step at least doubles its size, so at most s generators suffice. Pad to s entries with identities: there are at most `N^s` subgroups, and at most N left cosets per subgroup.

Consequently the total probability of failure is at most

`2 exp(-p^{1+a}/3) + N^{s+1}(64 e p^{a-1})^{p/2}`,            (4.3)

which tends to zero because `a<1`. Choose a realization avoiding all failures, and set `A=U union S`. It is symmetric, its cardinality satisfies (4.2), and every proper coset meets it in at most `2p`. Dividing by `|A|>=4p^{1+a}` proves the cap. A cannot be contained in a proper subgroup, so it generates G. Also `E(A)>=E(U)=p^3`, since all the ordered solutions from U remain solutions in A. This proves the bound for K_E.

If (4.1) held for `l=U`, then (4.2) would give

`p <= C 13^{10/3} p^{(1+10a)/3}`.

Since `(1+10a)/3<1`, this is impossible for all sufficiently large p. QED.

For `mu=u_A`, `t(mu)=|A|^{-1}-N^{-1}` has exponent `1+a`. Thus the family is in the requested norm range whenever

`h0 < 1+a < 2-2c`.

This includes the displayed sample entropy `h0=1/10`, both values `a in {1/24,1/30}`, and both target exponents. In particular the failed substitution cannot be rescued by excluding the above-norm-threshold Borel-mixture example.

More generally, for `D>=0`, an estimate of the form

`|A intersect U| <= C K_E(A)^D |A|^{1/3}`

uniformly for this family would require

`D >= (2-a)/(9a)`.

The necessary powers are `47/9` when `a=1/24`, and `59/9` when `a=1/30`. These numbers follow by comparing `1` with `3aD+(1+a)/3`; they are **not** claimed to be sufficient powers or sharp bounds for a complete flattening argument.

**What is obstructed.** Large energy can be supplied by a small structured part of A while much of A has no corresponding small-product-set control. A whole-set line estimate cannot simply inherit the tripling parameter from this energy. A successful proof could treat that structured contribution separately; Proposition 3 neither rules this out nor proves that a BSG extraction with its usual exponents is indispensable.

## 5. A precise conditional replacement lemma and its weighted transfer

Here is a concrete sufficient set statement. It remains unproved in the required generality.

**Set-energy hypothesis SE(eta,theta,C).** For every sufficiently large prime p and every nonempty symmetric `A subset G`,

`E(A)/|A|^3 <= C [ |A|^{-eta} + alpha(A)^theta + |A|/N ]`,   (5.1)

where `eta,theta>0` and C is independent of p and A.

The subgroup term and the density term are explicit. A statement mentioning only “not Borel-concentrated” is insufficient unless its quantitative meaning, other subgroup cases, and constants are specified.

**Proposition 4 (conditional conclusion; unconditional proof of the implication).** Assume (5.1). Let `0<h0<2`, `a>0`, and choose

`0<c< min{ eta*h0/[2(1+eta)], theta*a/(2+theta), 1/2 }`.      (5.2)

Then every symmetric probability measure satisfying the task's cap and norm interval obeys the requested flattening inequality for all sufficiently large p. The onset depends on the constants in (5.1) and the strict margins in (5.2). No BSG theorem is used in this transfer.

**Proof.** Let `q=||mu||_2^2` and suppose the desired conclusion fails. Put `t=t(mu)`. For `p>=3` the norm-range assumptions imply

`q>=p^{-2+2c}` and `q<=2p^{-h0}`, with `t>=q/2`.

Consequently

`||mu*mu||_2^2 > (1/2)p^{-2c}q =: B`.

For any function f define the Schatten-four norm

`Q(f) = ||f*tilde f||_2^{1/2}`,

so that `Q(f)^4=||f*tilde f||_2^2`. It is a norm: in Fourier space its fourth power is `N^{-1}sum_rho d_rho ||f_hat(rho)||_{S4}^4`. The direct-sum Schatten norm satisfies Minkowski. Symmetry ensures `Q(mu)^4=||mu*mu||_2^2`.

Set `delta=B/16=p^{-2c}q/32` and `mu_low=mu 1_{mu<delta}`. Young's inequality gives

`Q(mu_low)^4 <= ||mu_low||_1^2 ||mu_low||_2^2 <= delta=B/16`.

Partition the remainder into dyadic levels `A_j={Delta_j<=mu<2Delta_j}` with `Delta_j>=delta`. All A_j are symmetric. There are at most

`L = ceil(log_2(32p^2))+2`

levels, since `delta>=p^{-2}/32` and `mu<=1`. By the triangle inequality for Q, some `f_j=mu 1_{A_j}` has

`Q(f_j)^4 > B/(16L^4)`.

Writing `A=A_j`, `Delta=Delta_j`, `n=|A|`, pointwise domination `f_j<=2Delta 1_A` in the nonnegative energy sum gives

`Delta^4 E(A) > p^{-2c}q/(512L^4)=q/K`,                    (5.3)

where `K=512L^4p^{2c}`. Write `w=Delta n`, `s=Delta^2 n`. We have `w<=1`, `s<=q`, and `E(A)<=n^3`. The single inequality (5.3) now yields four useful bounds:

`w>=K^{-1/2}`,                                              (5.4)

`(Kq)^{-1}<n<K/q`,                                         (5.5)

`E(A)/n^3>1/K`,                                            (5.6)

`alpha(A)<=sqrt(K)p^{-a}`.                                  (5.7)

For completeness, `Delta^4 n^3=w^2s=s^2n=w^4/n`. Compare each form with `q/K`, using `s<=q` and `w<=1`, to obtain (5.4)–(5.6). For (5.7), `Delta|A intersect xH|<=mu(xH)<=p^{-a}` and divide by w.

By (5.5), the range of q, and `N=p^3-p`,

`n^{-eta} <= (1024L^4)^eta p^{-eta(h0-2c)}`,

`alpha(A)^theta <= (512L^4)^{theta/2} p^{-theta(a-c)}`,

`n/N <= 1024 L^4 p^{-1}`.

Hypothesis (5.1) contradicts (5.6) once

`C[(1024L^4)^eta p^{-eta(h0-2c)}`

`  +(512L^4)^{theta/2}p^{-theta(a-c)}+1024L^4p^{-1}]`

`              < p^{-2c}/(512L^4)`.                         (5.8)

The three strict conditions in (5.2) say exactly that the three powers on the left of (5.8) decay faster than `p^{-2c}`. Since L grows logarithmically, (5.8) holds for every sufficiently large p. This proves the implication. QED.

**Numerical meaning of the exponents.** Taking the hypothetical `eta=1/20`, `theta=1`, formula (5.2) becomes

`c < min{h0/42, a/3, 1/2}`.                                (5.9)

At `h0=1/10` and either `tau=1/4` or `tau=1/5`, the first term binds and is `1/420`. The specific announce-threshold exponent `1/468`, and also `1/450`, satisfy the inequalities with strict slack. This is only conditional on (5.1), which is the missing mathematics. By contrast `c=1/120` cannot be obtained from this particular transfer at `h0=1/10`; its entropy condition would require `h0>7/20`. This is a limitation of the proved sufficient criterion, not a counterexample to stronger flattening.

At `h0=1/8` and `h0=1/5`, the entropy terms are respectively `1/336` and `1/210`. None is the p-scale exponent `1/40` asserted by merely deleting the old BSG constants. An exponent expressed in powers of set size must be converted using the size of the energy-bearing level, and its mass may be as small as `p^{-c}` up to logarithms; (5.4) and (5.7) display the corresponding coset loss.

The displayed constants `16`, `32`, `512`, `1024`, and L are not optimized; their derivation is included to prevent a logarithmic factor being silently promoted to an endpoint exponent. The strict endpoints in (5.2) are essential to this proof. If C and the onset in (5.1) were supplied explicitly, (5.8) would give a checkable onset for the transfer.

## 6. Checks on symmetry and energy conventions

For symmetric mu, its dyadic level sets in §5 are symmetric, so **that extraction causes no symmetrization loss**. This does not prove that a small-tripling subset produced by an independent extraction is symmetric.

For a nonsymmetric mu, `||mu*mu||_2^2` need not equal `||mu*tilde mu||_2^2`. In Fourier space one always has

`||M^2||_HS^2 = tr((M* M)(M M*)) <= tr((M* M)^2)`,

by Hilbert–Schmidt Cauchy–Schwarz and equality of the singular-value lists of `M*M` and `MM*`. Summing over representations shows that the self-convolution energy is at most the correlation energy. Thus a nonsymmetric version of §5 could use a hypothesis (5.1) for **all** sets, but the version stated here assumes it only for symmetric sets and does not claim that extension. Proposition 1 and the coset-normalizer computation in §3 do not require symmetry.

The central subtraction has been retained throughout: (3.1) is exact, Proposition 1 discards only the trivial Fourier representation, and §5 compares q with t using the actual norm interval. No argument uses a false identity between `||mu||_2^2` and `t(mu)`.
