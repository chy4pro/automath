## 1. Correct target and quantifiers

Let p be an odd prime, I={1,...,N} in F_p, and let A=I dotplus Lambda_1, B=I dotplus Lambda_2 be nonempty direct sums. Thus these are unions of disjoint length-N cyclic intervals. Write a=|A|, b=|B|, alpha=a/p, beta=b/p, and

    C(A,B)=#{x in F_p^*: x in A, x^(-1) in B},
    D(A,B)=C(A,B)-ab/p.

The useful replacement for Corollary 16 is a TWO-SIDED bound

    |D(A,B)| <= C_* sqrt(ab) N^(-kappa_abs).                    (R3.1)

The task's stated upper bound C<=(1+N^(-kappa_0))ab/p alone does not give the lower bound used in Section 5.4. That application needs C(A,A)>=a^2/(2p), with the harmless p versus p-1 convention handled. The source's Corollary 16 proof, equations (35)-(36), has an O-error, and Section 5.4 explicitly subtracts that error to get a lower bound. [Shkredov, Corollary 16 and Section 5.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14116v2).

If alpha,beta>=p^(-eta) and N=p^(theta+o(1)), (R3.1) implies

    |D|/(ab/p) <= C_* (alpha beta)^(-1/2) N^(-kappa_abs)
                <= C_* p^(eta-theta*kappa_abs+o(1)).          (R3.2)

Consequently the coefficient-one relative target with exponent kappa_0 follows, for all sufficiently large p, from

    kappa_abs > kappa_0 + eta/theta.                         (R3.3)

The strict margin absorbs C_* and subpower factors. The losses are 90 eta at theta=1/90 and 9 eta at theta=1/9. With the task's eta<=1.98/M, a sufficient worst-case margin is 178.2/M or 17.82/M, respectively. Positivity alone needs theta*kappa_abs>eta. These inequalities are deductions, not improved estimates for w_M.

For fixed M, density p^(-eta) tends to zero. Calling this “near-full density” describes the cardinality exponent, not density tending to one. The exact centered norm is ||1_A-alpha||_2^2=a(1-alpha), which gives no uniform extra power of p under this hypothesis.

Throughout, power-saving statements mean fixed theta, eta and exponents, then p tending to infinity. No computed leading coefficient or starting prime is supplied by the cited bilinear theorems. The target in Section 5.4 is theta=1/90 approached from below, since N_*=p^(epsilon/5) and epsilon<1/18; a result at exactly 1/90 without a neighborhood would also need this endpoint issue resolved.

## 2. Exact unitary normalization

Use counting-measure L2 norms and the unitary Fourier transform

    fhat(xi)=p^(-1/2) sum_x f(x)e_p(-xi*x).

Extend inversion by 0^(-1)=0 and let Uf(x)=f(x^(-1)). This is a unitary involution fixing constants. If f=1_A-alpha and g=1_B-beta, then exactly

    D(A,B)=<f,Ug>-1_A(0)1_B(0).                              (R3.4)

For S(u,v;p)=sum_{x!=0} e_p(ux+v/x), the Fourier matrix is

    K_p(xi,zeta)=(S(-xi,zeta;p)+1)/p.                        (R3.5)

The +1 is necessary for the chosen zero convention. Its omission is a rank-one change. The zero frequency is excluded below because fhat(0)=ghat(0)=0. The matrix of normalized Kl_2 entries alone is not unitary: it requires a further division by sqrt(p) and the zero correction.

Let |xi|_p be the centered integer representative's absolute value, let P_X project to 0<|xi|_p<=X, for 1<=X<=floor(p/2), and define

    rho_p(X)=||P_X K_p P_X||_(2->2).

Unitarity gives rho_p(X)<=1. Weil and the Hilbert-Schmidt estimate give, with d_X=2 floor(X),

    rho_p(X) <= min{1, d_X(2 sqrt(p)+1)/p}.                 (R3.6)

In particular the ideal frequency width p/N yields only the factor O(sqrt(p)/N). At theta=1/9 and 1/90 this is p^(7/18) and p^(22/45), respectively, before taking the minimum with 1. A single incomplete rational exponential sum has completion bound O(sqrt(p) log p), whereas a single N by N hyperbola rectangle has error O(sqrt(p) log^2 p) against main term N^2/p. Their relative thresholds are 1/2 and 3/4; they answer different questions. These are method ceilings, not impossibility results. [Source normalization and completion discussion](../phase0/N4N5N6.md).

## 3. A proved Fourier-tail bridge, including the smoothing loss

**Lemma.** Under the interval-union hypotheses, for 1<=X<=floor(p/2),

    sum_{|xi|_p>X} |(1_A)hat(xi)|^2
         <= a (p/(NX)+2/N).                                (R3.7)

**Proof.** Put r=|Lambda_1|=a/N. Translation by an integer h>=1 changes a length-N interval in at most 2h points. By the union bound,

    ||1_A(.+h)-1_A||_2^2=|A triangle (A-h)|<=2rh.

Let H=ceil(p/X). For a nonzero frequency with |xi|_p>X, the geometric progression formula and sin(pi*t)>=2t for 0<=t<=1/2 give

    |H^(-1) sum_{h=1}^H e_p(h*xi)|
       <=1/(H |sin(pi*xi/p)|)<=p/(2H|xi|_p)<=1/2.

Thus H^(-1)sum_h |e_p(h*xi)-1|^2>=1. Parseval and averaging the preceding translation estimate imply

    sum_{|xi|_p>X}|(1_A)hat(xi)|^2
       <=H^(-1)sum_h 2rh=r(H+1)<=a(p/(NX)+2/N).

This also covers wrapping intervals and adjacent constituent intervals; adjacency can only reduce the boundary. No exact band-limit assumption is used. QED.

**Proposition.** With the same hypotheses,

    |D(A,B)| <= sqrt(ab) [rho_p(X)
                 +2 sqrt(p/(NX)+2/N)] +1.                 (R3.8)

**Proof.** Split fhat and ghat into P_X and its orthogonal complement in the nonzero frequencies. The low-low pairing is bounded by rho_p(X)sqrt(ab). Write the remainder as the high-f/full-g pairing plus the low-f/high-g pairing. Unitarity, Cauchy-Schwarz and (R3.7) bound each by sqrt(ab)sqrt(p/(NX)+2/N). Equation (R3.4) contributes at most 1. QED.

An immediate alternative is the trivial bound |D|<=sqrt(ab)+1; one can take the smaller bound. Formula (R3.8) is also valid with the exact centered norms in the low-frequency term.

Taking only X asymptotic to p/N leaves a constant-size tail. A genuine power saving by this reduction requires a wider block. Specifically, if N=p^(theta+o(1)) and

    X=p^(1-theta+u+o(1)),  0<u<theta,
    rho_p(X) << p^(-sigma),                                (R3.9)

then (R3.8) gives a saving p^(-gamma) for any

    gamma<min{sigma,u/2,theta/2},
    kappa_abs=gamma/theta.                                (R3.10)

The +1 is negligible for the requested dense sets. For arbitrary nonempty direct sums it is also negligible in this range because sqrt(ab)>=N and gamma<theta/2.

For a relative exponent kappa_0 on densities p^(-eta), the sufficient missing inputs are

    sigma>eta+theta*kappa_0,
    u>2eta+2theta*kappa_0,
    theta/2>eta+theta*kappa_0,                              (R3.11)

together with 2eta+theta*kappa_0<1 to absorb the zero-point term. This is an explicit sufficient bridge, not a proved equivalence between the dense-set problem and the arbitrary-coefficient operator problem.

## 4. What completion and an available analytic bound actually prove

Combining (R3.6) and (R3.8), optimizing X at scale p^(2/3)N^(-1/3), yields

    |D|/sqrt(ab) << min{1, p^(1/6)N^(-1/3)+N^(-1/2)}
                         +1/sqrt(ab).                     (R3.12)

Thus this fully justified completion route gives an exponent when theta>1/2, with p-saving (theta-1/2)/3. At the two target scales it gives no positive exponent. Unlike the ideal-block heuristic, this bound accounts for indicator tails.

There is a larger-interval improvement from [Blomer–Pascadi, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24311v1): for intervals of length L, arbitrary coefficients, and the unnormalized S kernel, their bound divided by p is

    p^o(1) [L^(1/8)p^(-3/32)+L^(5/16)p^(-3/16)
                                      +L^(2/3)p^(-7/18)].

Apply it to the interval [-X,X], setting the zero coefficients to zero. Since p is prime, the theorem's condition (m,n,p)=1 is automatic on these supports. The added all-ones matrix in (R3.5) contributes at most 2X/p. Consequently

    rho_p(X) << p^o(1)[X^(1/8)p^(-3/32)
             +X^(5/16)p^(-3/16)+X^(2/3)p^(-7/18)] +2X/p.   (R3.13)

**Proved consequence.** For every fixed 5/12<theta<1 and every

    0<gamma<G(theta):=
      min{(4theta-1)/40, (5theta-2)/26, (12theta-5)/42},     (R3.14)

all the stated disjoint interval unions with N=p^(theta+o(1)) satisfy

    |D(A,B)| <<_(theta,gamma) sqrt(ab) p^(-gamma)+1.

This is a deduction from the cited theorem, not a claim of a new literature record or a fully effective coefficient/onset.

**Calculation.** Substitute X=p^(1-theta+u) into (R3.13). The three prospective savings are

    s_1-u/8,      s_1=(4theta-1)/32;
    s_2-5u/16,    s_2=(5theta-2)/16;
    s_3-2u/3,     s_3=(12theta-5)/18.

The tail saving is u/2. Setting u=2G(theta) makes every term save at least G(theta). In this range u<theta and theta-u>G(theta), so the rank-one and N^(-1/2) terms are harmless. Taking gamma strictly below G absorbs p^o(1) and rounding. For example theta=1/2 permits any gamma<1/52, or any kappa_abs<1/26. This example concerns much longer physical intervals than R3 requires.

At the optimistic width X=p^(1-theta), even before allowing u>0, the three terms of (R3.13) have the following POSITIVE exponents:

| Physical theta | First | Second | Third |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1/9 | 5/288 | 13/144 | 11/54 |
| 1/90 | 43/1440 | 35/288 | 73/270 |

Widening the block only worsens these terms. This directly rules out obtaining the target by substituting the current balanced Blomer–Pascadi formula into this reduction.

## 5. Other source inputs, and the remaining obstruction

The precise KMS, Pascadi, Kerr–Shparlinski–Wu–Xi and Bourgain–Garaev statements and numerical exponents are recorded in [the N4 audit](../phase0/N4N5N6.md). Their application here has the following constraints.

- KMS's short Type-II threshold p^(3/8) concerns the dual summation length, not physical interval length N. The dual lengths here are at least p^(8/9) and p^(89/90). Saving against the entrywise bilinear bound also does not by itself save against the unitary norm 1.
- Pascadi's factorable-modulus improvement cannot be imposed on prime p. Its square-root-range bound and the stronger Blomer–Pascadi bound do not reach these long dual blocks.
- The short reciprocal phases e_p(am/k) and e_p(a/(mn)) are not the complete Kloosterman kernel. Their numerical savings cannot be inserted into (R3.9) without a new reduction that preserves coefficients and every factor of p.
- Subdividing a long block into short blocks introduces a cost: if each side is partitioned into r blocks and each subblock norm is <=b_0, the general bound is r*b_0, not b_0. This follows by summing pairings and applying Cauchy-Schwarz to each list of block norms. Density alone supplies no cancellation among the blocks.

I additionally checked [Fouvry–Kowalski–Michel–Sawin v3, Theorem 1.3(2)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09459v3). Its general trace-function Type-II estimate requires a summation length at most p^(1/2+3/(4ell)), ell>=2. Even the largest upper endpoint is p^(7/8), below p^(8/9), before the required smoothing enlargement. The theorem supplies no direct longer-block input here. Its multilinear variants require additional coefficient factorization not possessed by arbitrary interval unions.

A late source check changes the assessment of [Shkredov 2019, Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/html/1905.00291). It prints a saving p^(-delta(c)) against the S-kernel norm p when ONE support interval is at most p^(1-c). This literal statement is false. Fix a!=0, put v=1_{a}, and let w(m)=overline{S(a,m;p)} for all m. Orthogonality gives

    sum_m |S(a,m;p)|^2=p(p-1),
    |sum v(n)w(m)S(n,m;p)|/(||v||_2||w||_2)
       =sqrt(p(p-1)).

Indeed, opening the square makes the sum over m equal p when the two inverse variables agree and zero otherwise. This contradicts any p^(1-delta) bound, even with fixed logarithmic factors. There is also a zero-frequency issue: v=w=1_{0} gives p-1, including when both shifted support intervals are short.

Neither example refutes R3's balanced, zero-excluded block. Nevertheless, I did not repair the proof of Theorem 33 enough to verify that narrower operator claim. The earlier broad statement in task 002 has been corrected. The 2019 source is not used as a valid numerical or qualitative block-norm input in this report.

The already-available qualitative result needed to distinguish this task from mere existence is Corollary 16 itself: it supplies a positive but uncomputed centered-discrepancy exponent for the stated interval unions. The challenge is a verified numerical exponent at the campaign's thresholds. This does not prove a general arbitrary-coefficient block theorem, and it would be unjustified to assert that no published result whatsoever covers the long-block regime.

The useful unresolved statement is now exact: obtain (R3.9) with explicit sigma,u satisfying (R3.11), or estimate the low-frequency pairing only for the special coefficients arising from interval-union indicators, strongly enough to replace rho_p(X) in (R3.8). The latter is a weaker sufficient task. No argument here shows density makes those coefficients arbitrary, or that the two tasks are equivalent.

## 6. Negative list and campaign consequence

1. The relative exponent in task 005 is not the Corollary-16 exponent; the conversion loses eta/theta. For existence, prove a two-sided centered error or a lower bound.
2. Physical N and dual p/N must not be interchanged. The relevant blocks are long.
3. Indicators are not band limited. Formula (R3.7) quantifies the required enlargement and gives the exact loss.
4. Completion gives no target saving. This is a limitation of the estimate, not a counterexample to the target.
5. The current balanced analytic Type-II bounds give the explicit larger-interval range (R3.14), not theta=1/9 or 1/90.
6. Short reciprocal-phase estimates, Type-I estimates, and composite factorization gains do not transfer automatically.
7. The qualitative centered-discrepancy result already exists. The 2019 paper's related one-short-support bilinear statement has the counterexample above; a corrected balanced operator theorem is not verified here. Neither citation supplies a verified numerical exponent.
8. A point-plane or point-line incidence theorem would need an explicit conversion controlling this centered count with its density and coefficient norms. No such conversion was proved in this work; no generic incidence exponent is claimed as a plug-in.
9. A theorem only at the endpoint theta=1/90 would need extension to the smaller exponents actually used in Section 5.4.

At the target scales the best newly CERTIFIED explicit exponent from the direct analytic substitutions above is zero. This does not assert that the best true or published qualitative exponent is zero. Neither the 2^(-486) nor the 2^(-136) numerical target is certified.
