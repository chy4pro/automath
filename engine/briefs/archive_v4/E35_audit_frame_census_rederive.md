# Task: INDEPENDENT RE-DERIVATION of a published census, from its published specification only

You are being asked to recompute three published numbers from scratch. **You are given the
specification exactly as it appears in the published paper and NOTHING ELSE — no code, no context, no
statement of what we expect you to find.** That omission is deliberate: the entire value of this task is
that your derivation shares nothing with ours.

## What to compute
Over the **exhaustive `n ≤ 7` atlas of simple graphs, plus the `n = 8` cover** described below,
restricted to the **hard-core frame** (no reductio hypothesis imposed), report:

1. **The number of frame graphs.**
2. **The number of pairs realising the property labelled `Fact F-b` below.**
3. **The multiset of frame-graph counts by a parameter, in the form `{2: a, 3: b, 4: c}`.**

## The specification, quoted from the published paper
The paper states the census is taken over *"the exhaustive `n ≤ 7` atlas plus the `n = 8` cover,
restricted to the hard-core frame — no reductio hypothesis imposed"*, and reports counts of **frame
graphs**, of **pairs realising Fact F-b**, and a **breakdown by a small integer parameter**.

⚠️ **If the specification as quoted is NOT sufficient for you to determine unambiguously what a "frame
graph", the "hard-core frame", the "n = 8 cover", or "Fact F-b" means — SAY SO, PRECISELY, AND STOP.**
**That answer is at least as valuable as a number**, because it means our published text is not
re-derivable by a reader, which is a defect in the publication itself. **Do not guess a reading and
compute something; do not fill the gap from general knowledge of the area.** Name every ambiguity you
hit and say what you would need.

## If you can proceed
- State your interpretation of each term **before** computing with it.
- Report your numbers **whatever they are**. Do not round toward anything, and do not adjust an answer
  because it looks like it should be a rounder number.
- Give the counts, then a short description of your enumeration method and any cutoffs.
- If your enumeration is partial (e.g. you cover `n ≤ 6` exactly and estimate beyond), **say exactly
  where it becomes partial** rather than reporting a single total.

## Honest stopping is a valued answer
**"I could not finish, and here is exactly where I stopped" is a full and useful response, not a
failure.** On this project, three independent honest stopping-points once converged on the same missing
idea and that convergence produced six lemmas. A confident wrong number is far worse than a precise
account of where the specification ran out.
