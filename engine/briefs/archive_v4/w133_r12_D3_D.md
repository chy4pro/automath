PART 0 — DEFINITIONS (fixed for the whole problem; read them exactly)
====================================================================

All graphs are finite, simple, undirected and connected.

* **C4-free** means: no two distinct vertices have two or more common neighbours.
  (Equivalently, no 4-cycle as a SUBGRAPH; chords are irrelevant. TRIANGLES ARE ALLOWED
  — do not assume girth >= 5.)
* For a vertex `v`, `N(v)` is the OPEN neighbourhood and `a(v) := alpha(G[N(v)])`, the
  independence number of the subgraph induced on `N(v)`.
  **Fact F1.** In a C4-free graph `G[N(v)]` is a matching (a vertex of `N(v)` with two
  neighbours in `N(v)` would give a C4), hence `a(v) = d(v) - t(v)` where `d(v)` is the
  degree and `t(v)` is the number of triangles containing `v`. Components of `G[N(v)]`
  are therefore singletons or single edges, and their number is exactly `a(v)`.
* `l(G) := (1/n) * sum_v a(v)` where `n = |V(G)|`.  `mu(G) := min_v a(v)`.
* `path(G)` := the maximum number of VERTICES of an induced path of `G`. (So
  `path(G) >= 7` means "G contains an induced path on 7 vertices", i.e. an induced P7.)
* `rad`, `diam`, `dist` are the usual graph radius, diameter and distance.
* A **geodesic** `u_0 u_1 ... u_d` is a shortest path: `dist(u_i, u_j) = |i - j|` for all
  `i, j`.
* Let `u_0 u_1 ... u_d` be a geodesic. A vertex `x` is a **usable side-neighbour of
  `u_0`** if `x` is adjacent to `u_0` and `x` lies in a component of `G[N(u_0)]` OTHER
  than the component containing `u_1`. (By F1 this forces `x != u_1` and `x` not adjacent
  to `u_1`.) Symmetrically for `u_d` (component of `u_{d-1}` excluded); such a vertex is
  called a **usable far-side vertex** and written `y`.

  geodesic `u_0..u_d`, `d >= 5`, `a(u_0) >= 2`, `a(u_d) >= 2` and a usable side-neighbour
  `x` of `u_0` with `a(x) >= 4`, then `path(G) >= d + 4`.
  Proof sketch (you may re-use the construction): pick `y` in `N(u_d)` outside
  `u_{d-1}`'s component; pick `w` in `N(x)` in a component of `G[N(x)]` other than

  at-most-one common neighbour of `x` with `u_3` (this is what `a(x) >= 4` pays for).
  Then `w, x, u_0, u_1, ..., u_d, y` is induced: `d + 4` vertices.
* **F3 (d = 4).** Same statement at `d = 4` with `a(u_0) >= 3`, `a(u_d) >= 2`,
  `a(x) >= 4`: `path(G) >= 8`. (Here `a(u_0) >= 3` IS needed — the proof selects a second
  side-neighbour `z` of `u_0` outside both `u_1`'s and `x`'s components.)
* **F4 (d = 3, "Case 1").** Let `u_0 u_1 u_2 u_3` be a geodesic in a C4-free `G` with
  `a(u_0) >= 3`, `a(u_3) >= 2` and a usable side-neighbour `x` of `u_0` with
  `a(x) >= 4`. **If there exists a usable far-side vertex `y` of `u_3` with `y` NOT
  adjacent to `x`, then `path(G) >= 7`.**
* **F5 (sharpening of the remaining case; machine-verified on all 1112 qualifying local
  skeletons).** In a `3`-frame in which **every** usable far-side vertex `y` of `u_3` is
  adjacent to `x` (we call such a frame **Case-2-forced**), the set of usable far-side
  vertices is EXACTLY one vertex `{y}`, `dist(u_0, y) = 2`, and `u_0 - x - y - u_3` is
  itself a geodesic of length 3.
  *(Reason the set is a singleton: any usable far-side vertex adjacent to `x` lies in
  `N(x) cap N(u_3)`, which has size <= 1 by C4-freeness.)*
* **F6 (the C6 lemma; unconditional).** In ANY Case-2-forced `3`-frame the six vertices
  `u_0, u_1, u_2, u_3, y, x` induce a **6-cycle** `u_0 - u_1 - u_2 - u_3 - y - x - u_0`.

  the `u`'s form a geodesic; `x u_1` and `y u_2` fail by usability; `x u_2` would create
  the 4-cycle `x-u_0-u_1-u_2-x`; `y u_1` would create the 4-cycle `y-u_3-u_2-u_1-y`;
  `x u_3` and `y u_0` fail by distance.)* This lemma uses NO hypothesis on any a-value.
