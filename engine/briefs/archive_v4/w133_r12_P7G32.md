# PART 1 — DEFINITIONS

Graphs are finite, simple, undirected, connected.
* **C4-free**: no two distinct vertices have two or more common neighbours. (Equivalently
  no 4-cycle as a subgraph, chords irrelevant.) **Triangles are allowed.**
* `N(v)` = open neighbourhood; `a(v) := alpha(G[N(v)])` = independence number of `G[N(v)]`;
  `d(v)` = degree; `t(v)` = number of triangles containing `v`.
* **Fact F1.** C4-freeness makes `G[N(v)]` a matching plus isolated vertices, so
  `a(v) = d(v) - t(v)`, and `G[N(v)]` has exactly `a(v)` components, each a single vertex
  or a single edge.
* `l(G) := (1/n) sum_v a(v)`, `n = |V(G)|`; `mu(G) := min_v a(v)`.
* `path(G)` := number of **vertices** of a longest induced path. An **induced P_k** is a
  chordless path on `k` vertices. "**No induced P7**" means `path(G) <= 6`.
* A geodesic is a shortest path, hence always induced, so `path(G) >= diam(G) + 1`.

# PART 2 — GIVEN FACTS (proved elsewhere; use as black boxes)

**F2 (C4-freeness alone).** Let `Z = (z_0, …, z_5)` be an induced 6-cycle and `w` a vertex
outside `Z`. Then `w` has at most 2 neighbours on `Z`, and if exactly 2 they are either
**consecutive** on `Z` (cyclic distance 1) or **antipodal** (cyclic distance 3).
*(Cyclic distance 2 is impossible: the two neighbours would have both `w` and the vertex
between them as common neighbours, a C4.)*

**F3 (the P6 version — the lemma this brief asks you to upgrade).** If in addition `G` has
**no induced P6**, the consecutive case is impossible: if `w ~ z_0, z_1` and `w` has no
other `Z`-neighbour, then `w, z_0, z_5, z_4, z_3, z_2` is an induced P6.

**F4 (Theorem G+).** `G` connected C4-free with `mu(G) >= 2` and some `h` with
`a(h) >= 3` satisfies `path(G) >= ecc(h) + 3`.

**F5 (peeling).** Deleting a vertex with `a = 1` (a leaf, or a degree-2 vertex whose two
neighbours are adjacent) from a connected C4-free graph keeps it connected and C4-free,
does not increase `path`, and **raises `sum_v a(v) - 3n` by at least 1**.

**F6 (a reduction you may use).** In a connected C4-free graph with `l > 3` and
`path <= 6`, peeling to a fixpoint yields `G'` with `mu(G') >= 2`, `l(G') > 3`,
`path(G') <= 6`; no vertex of an induced C6 is ever peeled (each has two non-adjacent
cycle-neighbours); and then by F4 **every** vertex `h` of `G'` with `a(h) >= 3` has
`ecc(h) <= 3`, so `rad(G') <= 3` and `diam(G') <= 5`.

conclusion "`W` is empty" is **not available** — and it is genuinely false, see PART 4.

* Can two vertices of `W` attach at the **same** consecutive slot `{z_i, z_{i+1}}`? At
  **overlapping** slots? At **disjoint** slots? At **antipodal** slots? Decide each.
* Must every `w in W` have a neighbour inside `Z ∪ W`? Must `W ∪ Z` induce something
  restricted?
* Bound `|W|`. Bound `a(w)` for `w in W`. Bound `a(z_i)`.
* What about vertices at distance 2 from `Z` — can a vertex of `W` have a neighbour
  outside `Z ∪ W` at all, and how many?
