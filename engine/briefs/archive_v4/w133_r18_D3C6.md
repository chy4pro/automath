PART 0 — DEFINITIONS (fixed for the whole problem; read them exactly)
====================================================================

All graphs are finite, simple, undirected and connected.

* **C4-free** means: no two distinct vertices have two or more common neighbours.
  (Equivalently, no 4-cycle as a SUBGRAPH; chords are irrelevant. TRIANGLES ARE ALLOWED
  — do not assume girth >= 5.)
* For a vertex `v`, `N(v)` is the OPEN neighbourhood and `a(v) := alpha(G[N(v)])`, the
  independence number of the subgraph induced on `N(v)`.
  **Fact F1.** In a C4-free graph `G[N(v)]` is a matching (a vertex of `N(v)` with two
  neighbours in `N(v)` would give a C4), hence `a(v) = d(v) - t(v)` where `d(v)` is the
  degree and `t(v)` is the number of triangles containing `v`. Components of `G[N(v)]`
  are therefore singletons or single edges, and their number is exactly `a(v)`.
* `l(G) := (1/n) * sum_v a(v)` where `n = |V(G)|`.  `mu(G) := min_v a(v)`.
* `path(G)` := the maximum number of VERTICES of an induced path of `G`. So
  `path(G) >= 7` means "G contains an induced path on 7 vertices" (an induced P7), and
  "`G` has no induced P7" means `path(G) <= 6`.
  **WARNING, a real error that killed two earlier attempts on this exact problem:** the
  longest induced path OF an induced `C_k` has `k - 1` vertices, not `k`. An induced C6
  therefore certifies `path >= 5`, NOT `path >= 6`. Count the vertices of every induced
  path you build, one at a time, from the printed edge list.
* `rad`, `diam`, `dist`, `ecc` are the usual graph radius, diameter, distance and
  eccentricity.
* A **geodesic** `u_0 u_1 ... u_d` is a shortest path: `dist(u_i, u_j) = |i - j|`.
* Let `u_0 u_1 ... u_d` be a geodesic. A vertex `x` is a **usable side-neighbour of
  `u_0`** if `x ~ u_0` and `x` lies in a component of `G[N(u_0)]` OTHER than the
  component containing `u_1`. Symmetrically at `u_d`, where such a vertex is called a
  **usable far-side vertex** and written `y`.
* A **3-frame** is a C4-free `G` together with a geodesic `u_0 u_1 u_2 u_3`, a usable
  side-neighbour `x` of `u_0` with `a(x) >= 4`, and `a(u_0) >= 3`, `a(u_3) >= 2`.
  The frame is **Case-2-forced** if EVERY usable far-side vertex `y` of `u_3` is adjacent
  to `x`.
