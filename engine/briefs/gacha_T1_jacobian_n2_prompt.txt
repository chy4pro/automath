Research problem. Work from first principles. Do NOT search the web. Before attacking,
spend a moment recalling from memory every technique you know that is relevant to
polynomial maps of the affine plane, then use the best of them; show complete reasoning.

Let f, g be polynomials in C[x, y] and suppose the determinant

    J = (df/dx)(dg/dy) - (df/dy)(dg/dx)

is a nonzero constant. Let F(x, y) = (f(x, y), g(x, y)) : C^2 -> C^2.

Statement (S): every such F is bijective, with an inverse that is again polynomial.

Attempt BOTH directions, then commit to the one you find more promising:
(a) Prove (S).
(b) Construct an explicit pair (f, g) with J a nonzero constant for which F is NOT
    injective. Give the two polynomials in full, plus two distinct points with the same
    image, in exact arithmetic, so the failure can be checked mechanically.

Constraints you may use, they are correct and they say where a counterexample must live:
 - The analogous statement in dimension >= 3 over C is FALSE; explicit counterexamples were
   found in 2026. The plane case is what is asked here and is not settled by them.
 - Over a field of characteristic p the analogous statement fails for trivial reasons
   (x -> x^p has vanishing derivative), so characteristic 0 is essential; do not answer
   with a characteristic-p example.
 - It suffices to treat F = identity + H with H homogeneous of a single degree d >= 2,
   after a standard reduction; you may work in that normal form.

Partial results are valuable and welcome, in this order of value to me:
 1. an exact degree bound: a proof of (S) for all f, g of degree <= d for some d you can
    actually reach, with the argument written out;
 2. a structural obstruction: a proof that no counterexample of a named shape can exist
    (for example: no counterexample with H homogeneous of degree 3, or none whose
    components have coprime degrees);
 3. a reduction that makes the general plane case finite-checkable, with the finite check
    stated precisely enough to be programmed.

End with a clear list: PROVED (with the proofs written out) vs CONJECTURED (labelled as
such). If you produce any explicit polynomials, state them in a form that can be copied
into a computer algebra system verbatim.
