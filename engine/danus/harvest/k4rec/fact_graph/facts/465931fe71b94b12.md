## statement
Let p=2, let F be an algebraic closure of GF(2), let n=m2^e with m odd, and write positions modulo n. For every even k with 2≤k≤n, the all-ones multiset has a clean placement of k distinct positions in Z_n. Explicitly, choose g coprime to n, set r=k/2, and place pairs at {jg,jg+g} for j=0,...,r-1 modulo n. This placement is clean; in fact its polynomial is (1+x^g)(1+x^g+x^{2g}+...+x^{(r-1)g}), which is nonzero at every λ∈μ_m\{1}.

## proof
Choose any integer g with gcd(g,n)=1 (for example a prime not dividing n if necessary; more simply take g=1 when n is odd, while for even n take g an odd integer coprime to n). Let r=k/2. Consider the cycle obtained from Z_n by adding edges {a,a+g}. Since g generates Z_n, this is one n-cycle. Its vertices may be listed as 0,g,2g,...,(n-1)g. The 2r required vertices 0,g,2g,...,(2r-1)g are distinct because 2r=k≤n. Pair consecutive vertices in this list as {jg,(j+1)g} for j=0,...,r-1. These are k distinct positions.

The all-ones polynomial at these positions is
P(x)=Σ_{j=0}^{r-1}(x^{jg}+x^{(j+1)g})=(1+x^g)Σ_{j=0}^{r-1}x^{jg}.

Let λ∈μ_m. Since gcd(g,m)=1, λ^g=1 iff λ=1. Hence if λ≠1, then 1+λ^g≠0, so P(λ)≠0. Therefore no nontrivial m-th root is a root of P, and in particular no reciprocal pair λ,λ^{-1} both annihilate P. Thus the placement is clean.

## intuition
A common pair factor 1+x^g can be forced to have no nontrivial roots among the relevant odd-order roots by taking g coprime to n. A matching along the generated cycle supplies enough disjoint pairs for every even k.
