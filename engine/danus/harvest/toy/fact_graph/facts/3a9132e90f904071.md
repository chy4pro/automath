## statement
For every positive integer \(n\), the sum of the first \(n\) positive integers equals \(n(n+1)/2\). That is,
\[
1 + 2 + 3 + \cdots + n = \frac{n(n+1)}{2} \qquad \text{for all } n \ge 1.
\]

## proof
Let
\[
S_n=\sum_{k=1}^{n} k=1+2+\cdots+n
\]
for each positive integer \(n\). We prove \(S_n=n(n+1)/2\) by mathematical induction on \(n\).

Base case \(n=1\): the left-hand side is \(S_1=1\), while
\[
\frac{1(1+1)}{2}=\frac{2}{2}=1.
\]
Thus the formula holds when \(n=1\).

Induction step: suppose for some positive integer \(n\) that
\[
S_n=\sum_{k=1}^{n} k=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}.
\]
Then, using associativity and commutativity of addition in the integers followed by the induction hypothesis,
\[
S_{n+1}
=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1} k
=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n} k\right)+(n+1)
=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}+(n+1).
\]
Since \(n(n+1)/2=(n(n+1))/2\) and \((n+1)=(2(n+1))/2\),
\[
S_{n+1}
=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}+\frac{2(n+1)}{2}
=\frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2}.
\]
This is exactly the claimed formula with \(n+1\) in place of \(n\). Therefore, if the formula holds at \(n\), it holds at \(n+1\).

By the principle of mathematical induction, the formula holds for every positive integer \(n\):
\[
1 + 2 + 3 + \cdots + n = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}\qquad\text{for all }n\ge1.
\]

The arXiv result listed as Lemma 6.3.1(1) of arXiv:2403.10430 states this same identity; it is cited only as corroborating context. The proof above is complete from first principles and does not rely on that source.

## intuition
Standard induction on n: after assuming the triangular-number formula for n, adding n+1 factors into the corresponding formula for n+1.
